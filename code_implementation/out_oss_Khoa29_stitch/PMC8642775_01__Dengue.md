This case was firstly suspicious for a possible vaccine adverse reaction. Nevertheless, there is no coagulation profile abnormality. The patient presented with myalgia, fever, and a skin rash, and received a viral-vector COVID-19 vaccine two days prior. However, the absence of coagulation abnormalities and the endemic context of viral hemorrhagic diseases in the region lowers the likelihood of a vaccine-related adverse reaction.

Further investigation for viral hemorrhagic disease was performed. The patient's symptoms, such as fever and rash, and isolated thrombocytopenia, are consistent with a viral hemorrhagic etiology. In fact, the patient's laboratory evaluation showed a decreased platelet count of 80 × 10³/µL, which is a key finding in viral hemorrhagic diseases.

Further laboratory tests confirm that there is a positive result of dengue virus infection (Dengue NS1Ag + ve). This definitive diagnosis confirms the suspicion of a viral hemorrhagic disease, and specifically points towards dengue infection as the most likely cause of the patient's symptoms.
