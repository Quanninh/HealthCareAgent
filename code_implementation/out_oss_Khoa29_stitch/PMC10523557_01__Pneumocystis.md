The patient, a 6-month-old baby girl of Swedish descent, was admitted with severe pneumonia. Her prior records showed failure-to-thrive and thrombocytosis. She required respiratory support, corticosteroids, and antibiotics in the intensive care unit. Nasal swabs were positive for rhinovirus and bocavirus, and chest radiography was interpreted as suggestive of a bacterial infection. However, due to halting recovery after a week, extended testing was performed. The detected respiratory viruses, rhinovirus and bocavirus, are usually associated with mild illness and do not explain severe, refractory pneumonia in this infant. Initial chest imaging suggested bacterial pneumonia, but the persistent disease and later microbiologic evidence favored a different etiology. A nasopharyngeal swab PCR positive for Pneumocystis jirovecii (PCJ) directly indicated a fungal opportunistic infection. Confirmatory radiologic and microbiologic findings substantiated the diagnosis of Pneumocystis pneumonia.
