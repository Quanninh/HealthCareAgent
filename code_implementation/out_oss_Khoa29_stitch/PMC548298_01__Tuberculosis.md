The patient is a 23-year-old man with polymyositis being treated with immunosuppressive therapy, which predisposes him to opportunistic infections, prompting evaluation for atypical mycobacterial or fungal disease. He was HIV-negative, his chest X-ray was normal, and there was no acid-fast bacilli in three consecutive sputum samples (spot-early-morning-spot, induced on two occasions). This absence of acid-fast bacilli in serial sputum specimens argues against active pulmonary tuberculosis at this stage, as there was no AFB in three consecutive (spot-early morning-spot) sputum samples.

However, the presence of a single cutaneous abscess on the right side of the neck, without sinus formation, accompanied by cervical and axillary lymphadenopathy, requires further investigation. The histopathology of the biopsy specimen shows nonspecific necrosis with disintegrating polymorphs, very few granulomatous cells, and no epithelioid cells, which does not support a typical bacterial abscess or a granulomatous fungal infection. This finding further points toward tuberculosis.

To confirm the diagnosis, culture of the aspirated pus and axillary lymph-node biopsy was performed, which subsequently yielded abundant Mycobacterium tuberculosis, confirming extrapulmonary tuberculosis. The aspirated pus from the cutaneous abscess and biopsy of the axillary lymph node showed plenty of M. tuberculosis as culture was confirmed by niacin and nitrate reduction tests, rapid bacteriophage assay, and RFLP analysis.

Based on the evidence, the final diagnosis is tuberculosis.
