This is further supported by the fact that immunologic abnormalities persisted despite anti-toxoplasma therapy. Chronic toxoplasmosis is considered due to high-titer IgG/IgM and PCR-positive lymph node, but immunologic abnormalities persisted despite anti-toxoplasma therapy, suggesting an underlying immunodeficiency.

Given the patient's specific antibody responses to pneumococcal polysaccharides were not protective to all serotypes, common variable immunodeficiency (CVID) is entertained. This is a significant finding, as it suggests a defect in the humoral immune response.

The patient's elevated IgM levels and lack of switched memory B cells are also notable. This pattern is characteristic of hyper-IgM syndrome, but the normal IgG levels and the presence of expanded T-follicular helper cells are inconsistent with classic X-linked or autosomal-dominant hyper-IgM. 

Other primary immunodeficiencies, such as STAT3-GOF and ADA deficiency, were excluded by the specific B-cell phenotype (high transitional B cells, low memory B cells) and T-cell subset distribution. Analysis of lymphocyte subsets revealed decreased CD20⁺ CD27⁺ memory B cells and an increased proportion of CD19⁺ CD10⁺ transitional B cells (36.4% of total B cells).

The combination of these findings points towards a specific underlying immunodeficiency. Whole exome gene sequencing identified a heterozygous mutation at an essential donor splice site of PIK3R1, which leads to PI3K pathway hyperactivation and recapitulates the observed immunophenotype. This is consistent with Activated PI3K-Delta syndrome type 2.
