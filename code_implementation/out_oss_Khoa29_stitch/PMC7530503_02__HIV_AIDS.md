The patient presents with a non-pruritic erythematous maculopapular rash that began on his face and spread to his trunk, arms, legs, palms, and soles, accompanied by headaches, photosensitivity, and intermittent fevers. The initial workup for viral meningitis yielded negative results, and the clinical picture was most consistent with a viral exanthem. However, this diagnosis is later ruled out by the lack of sustained viral serology positivity.

The patient's rash partially resolves, but he returns with worsening of the same non-pruritic rash accompanied by neck pain, photophobia, headache, low-back pain, fatigue, anorexia, and intermittent fevers up to 102°F. Examination shows generalized, well-defined, non-pruritic, non-blanching confluent targetoid papules and plaques in various stages of healing on the torso, back, arms, legs, palms, soles, and lips; newer lesions are erythematous, older lesions darker, and lesions on forearms and face exhibit fine scaling. No mucosal involvement is noted.

Infectious etiologies such as HBV, HCV, HSV-1/2, mycoplasma, EBV, HIV, and parvovirus B19 are systematically excluded by negative serologic testing. The lesions resemble erythema multiforme, but this diagnosis is excluded because the lesions are non-blanching, there is no mucosal involvement, and serologies for common triggers (HSV, Mycoplasma) are negative. The lesions, resembling EM, have newer lesions appearing erythematous, while older lesions appear darker, and infectious workup, including HSV 1 and 2, mycoplasma, was negative.

A drug-induced cutaneous reaction is considered but not supported by medication history and by the presence of high-titer autoantibodies (ANA >1:1280, anti-Ro/SSA, anti-La/SSB). Positive ANA titer of >1:1280, positive anti-Ro/SSA, anti-La/SSB titers of >8.0, and the presence of lupus anticoagulant antibody was positive.

The combination of photosensitivity, positive ANA, anti-Ro/SSA, anti-La/SSB, lupus anticoagulant, and antiphospholipid antibodies, together with the clinical presentation, favors systemic lupus erythematosus (cutaneous lupus) as the diagnosis.
