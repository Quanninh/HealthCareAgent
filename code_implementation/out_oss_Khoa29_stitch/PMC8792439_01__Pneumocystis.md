A 27-year-old woman presented with a 10-year history of persistent weakness in both upper and lower limbs that had not interfered with her daily activities. She was misdiagnosed with seronegative polymyositis and received 2.5 milligrams prednisone per day one year prior to admission, but steroids provided no improvement and the weakness persisted despite treatment. This led us to consider alternative explanations for her symptoms.

She had hypoglycemia, which raised the possibility of glycogen storage disease. However, the lack of definitive tissue evidence and inability to perform urine organic acid analysis limited our ability to confirm this diagnosis.

We also considered infectious etiologies, such as Cytomegalovirus, Herpes simplex virus, Epstein-Barr virus, and Pneumocystis, but next-generation sequencing for infectious pathogens and culture of bronchoalveolar lavage fluid were performed, and no pathogen was isolated.

Thrombotic microangiopathy was excluded because peripheral smear findings were not consistent with this diagnosis, and pathogenic mutations in genes such as CD46, CFI, CFB, C3, THBD, and CFH were absent.

Acute fatty liver of pregnancy (AFLP) and HELLP syndrome were dismissed after confirming that the patient was not pregnant.

Oxidative hemolysis, such as G6PD deficiency, was excluded by normal peripheral smear findings and lack of pathogenic G6PD mutations on exome sequencing.

Primary (genetic) hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis was ruled out because sequencing showed no mutations in PRF1, UNC13D, STXBP2, STX1, RAB27A, LYST, AP3B1, SH2D1A, or XIAP.

Malignancy and autoimmune disorders were not identified despite extensive work-up, supporting a secondary trigger for HLH.

Whole-exome sequencing identified a homozygous ETFDH c.250G>A mutation, confirming glutaric aciduria type IIC. This finding was significant, as it explained the patient's symptoms and led us to consider a diagnosis of secondary HLH.

Hemophagocytosis was observed on a bone marrow biopsy, and the patient met six of the HLH criteria, including persistent fever, splenomegaly, cytopenias, increased ferritin, hypertriglyceridemia, and hypofibrinogenemia. These findings suggested a diagnosis of secondary HLH.

In conclusion, the patient's symptoms and laboratory findings were consistent with a diagnosis of glutaric aciduria type IIC and secondary hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis.
