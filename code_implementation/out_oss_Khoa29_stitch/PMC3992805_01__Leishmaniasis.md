The patient, a 50-year-old male agriculturist with a history of HIV, presented with asymptomatic skin lesions on his limbs. Considering the patient's immunocompromised state, histoplasmosis was considered as a potential cause, as disseminated histoplasmosis can present with papulonodular skin lesions in advanced HIV infection. However, histoplasmosis was excluded when the skin biopsy did not support it. 

Cutaneous leishmaniasis was identified as a potential cause, as it can present with similar skin lesions, despite the lack of travel to endemic regions. The punch biopsy of skin lesions showed sheets of histiocytic cells in the subepithelial tissue, stuffed with numerous amastigotes of Leishmania, confirming the diagnosis.
