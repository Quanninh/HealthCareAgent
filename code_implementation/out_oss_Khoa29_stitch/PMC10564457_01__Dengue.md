Initially, dengue fever was suspected because of fever and rash, but the detection of NS-1 antigen yielded negative results. This led to the consideration of bacterial sepsis due to fever, hypotension, and multiorgan involvement. However, the lack of an identified pathogen and rapid progression despite broad-spectrum antibiotics made sepsis less likely. Empiric antibiotics administration (ceftriaxone 2 g/day and doxycycline 200 mg/day) was also started due to suspicion of sepsis.

The hospital epidemiological surveillance team suspected that the patient was suffering from COVID-19-related multisystem inflammatory syndrome. This was proposed because of recent SARS-CoV-2 infection and the constellation of rash, shock, cardiac dysfunction, and elevated inflammatory state. The medical team identified a reverse-transcriptase PCR to COVID-19 positive in December 2022, and the patient reported a COVID-19 diagnosis on December 19, 2022, providing the necessary temporal association for a post-infectious inflammatory syndrome.

At this moment, the macrophage activation syndrome was also considered, although the patient had been presenting only fever, without hematological abnormalities or organomegaly. However, the combination of persistent fever, skin rash, conjunctivitis, shock, and echocardiographic evidence of myocardial hypokinesia, in the setting of a recent COVID-19 infection, fulfilled criteria for MIS-A, leading to the final diagnosis.
