The patient's presentation of persistent nocturnal and rotational neck pain radiating to the upper limbs, accompanied by a feeling of heaviness and paresthesia in the fingers, along with fever, fatigue, and headaches, raises suspicion for a neurological condition. The physical examination reveals pain on anteroposterior and lateral flexion of the neck with right latero-torticollis and dysesthesia at the C3-C4 level on the right, but cranial nerves, cerebellar testing, motor strength, gait, and sphincter function are normal. Magnetic resonance imaging of the cervical spine demonstrates a C1-C7 centromedullary T2-weighted hyper-signal with a normal T1 signal, slight cord swelling, and leptomeningeal enhancement at the lumbosacral and cauda equina levels, suggestive of longitudinal extensive transverse myelitis (LETM).

Given the diagnosis of LETM, demyelinating diseases such as NMOSD or MOG-associated disorder are considered, but both anti-aquaporin-4 and MOG antibodies were negative in the workup. However, this does not rule out other autoimmune inflammatory causes, as an extensive workup was performed, including serologies (Mycoplasma, Treponema, Hepatitis, Borrelia, Herpes, HIV, West Nile), autoimmune panels (antinuclear factor, ANCA, anti-ganglioside, rheumatoid factor), MOG and AQP4 antibodies, onconeural antibodies, and CSF analysis with a viral meningitis/encephalitis PCR panel. CSF showed pleocytosis (268 cells/µL), 5.6 RBC/µL, and markedly elevated protein (2.36 g/L); the meningitis/encephalitis PCR panel was negative.

Infectious etiologies were systematically evaluated, and viral meningitis/encephalitis was excluded because the CSF PCR panel was negative. Bacterial meningitis was also unlikely given the negative CSF bacterial culture and lack of sustained response to ceftriaxone alone. CSF bacterial culture was negative, and, therefore, Ceftriaxone was stopped. However, autoimmune inflammatory causes (ANCA-associated vasculitis, systemic lupus, etc.) were considered but excluded by negative antinuclear factor, ANCA, anti-ganglioside, and rheumatoid factor.

Considering the patient's residence in a tick-endemic area and the characteristic clinical picture, tick-borne infection (Borrelia) was entertained. Subsequent positive Borrelia IgG in serum and CSF confirmed neuroborrelial transverse myelitis. Blood Borrelia IgG were positive, confirmed by Western blot, and CSF Borrelia IgG test was done, which showed positive results, confirming the diagnosis of neuroborrelial TM.
