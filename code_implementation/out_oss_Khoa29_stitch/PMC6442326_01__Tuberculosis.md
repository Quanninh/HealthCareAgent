Considering the orbital mass with a plasma cell infiltrate, IgG4-related disease is a potential diagnosis. However, the immunoglobulin G (IgG) 4/IgG ratio was <5%, which against the diagnosis of IgG4-related disease. This suggests that IgG4-related disease is unlikely.

Another consideration is Langerhans cell histiocytosis, but immunohistochemistry study revealed that the histiocytes were positive for CD68 and S100, and negative for CD1a and langerin. This lack of CD1a and langerin expression makes Langerhans cell histiocytosis less likely.

Infectious granuloma, such as tuberculosis or fungal infections, are also possible, but special stains for acid-fast bacilli (Z-N) and fungi (periodic acid-Schiff and Gomori methenamine silver) were negative. This rules out infectious granuloma as a diagnosis.

Additionally, the presence of lymphoma or plasma-cell neoplasm is considered, but the plasma cells expressed CD79a, CD138, and appeared polyclonal with Kappa and Lambda light chain stains. This polyclonal staining pattern suggests that lymphoma or plasma-cell neoplasm is unlikely.

The most distinctive feature of this case is the presence of emperipolesis, where some of the histiocytes appeared to engulf small lymphocytes and plasma cells. This, combined with the histiocytes being positive for CD68 and S100, and negative for CD1a and langerin, is characteristic of Rosai-Dorfman disease.
