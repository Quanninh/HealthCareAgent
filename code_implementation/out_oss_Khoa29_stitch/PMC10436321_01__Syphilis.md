The patient's presentation of fever of unknown origin (FUO) and prosthetic aortic valve replacement raises concern for culture-negative prosthetic valve endocarditis. However, the transthoracic echocardiogram did not show vegetation or valvular dysfunction, and repeated cultures remained negative, making this diagnosis less likely. The transesophageal cardiography detected the presence of numerous floating prosthetic strands, but no evidence of vegetation was found.

Given the negative cultures and echocardiography results, we consider the possibility of a systemic bacterial infection caused by other pathogens. Tests for Legionella pneumophila, Treponema pallidum, Coxsackie virus A and B, Parvovirus B19, Leishmania spp., Toxoplasma gondii, HIV, and Hepatitis B and C were negative, arguing against these agents as the cause of the patient's symptoms.

The patient's laboratory studies revealed anemia, moderate cytopenia, elevated erythrocyte sedimentation rate, polyclonal hypergammaglobulinemia, thrombocytopenia, and mildly increased C-reactive protein and procalcitonin, which could be indicative of an infectious or autoimmune process. The patient's symptoms, including FUO, prosthetic valve replacement, and splenic and periaortic PET uptake, are consistent with this diagnosis.
