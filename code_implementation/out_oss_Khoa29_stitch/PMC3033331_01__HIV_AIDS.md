Considering the patient's presentation of unilateral visual loss, optic disc swelling, and serous retinal detachment in the left eye, an HIV test was ordered. HIV-serology was positive, suggesting an underlying immunodeficiency. However, the ocular finding was papillitis rather than typical HIV-related retinitis, which could predispose to opportunistic ocular infections. Opportunistic viral pathogens such as JC virus, HSV-1/2, VZV, and CMV can cause optic nerve inflammation, yet PCR testing of CSF for these agents was negative. This reduces the likelihood of these etiologies.

Bacterial, fungal, and mycobacterial infections were also considered, but cultures from all sites returned negative, further reducing the likelihood of such etiologies. The constellation of oral/genital ulcers, lymphadenopathy, hepatosplenomegaly, and central nervous system involvement suggests a systemic infection. Syphilis was suspected due to this constellation of symptoms. Serologic tests revealed a reactive Rapid Plasma Reagin (RPR)-titer of 1:32, a T. pallidum haemagglutination assay (TPHA) titer of 1:2560, and PCR detection of Treponema pallidum DNA from lesions confirmed active infection.

This distinguishes it from other causes of optic neuropathy. The therapeutic response to ceftriaxone, with normalization of sodium levels, healing of ulcers, and improvement of visual acuity, reinforces syphilis as the causative agent.
