The patient's presentation with multiple nodules and cystic-appearing tumors on the dorsum of his right foot and leg, following a sporotrichosis-like, linear distribution, suggests sporotrichosis as a potential diagnosis. However, the absence of a penetrating injury and the identification of pigmented hyphae rather than the typical hyaline yeast of Sporothrix makes this diagnosis unlikely. The lesions exhibited a sporotrichosis-like distribution pattern but there was no history of local trauma.

Another consideration is paracoccidioidomycosis, given the presence of cutaneous nodules. However, the culture yielded septate, pigmented hyphae with annellophores, which are not characteristic of the yeast-like morphology of Paracoccidioides spp. Culture on Sabouraud-Agar with chloramphenicol grew vegetative, chambered hyphae with septate annellophores, a pattern compatible with Exophiala species.

Leishmaniasis is also entertained due to the chronic skin lesions in an immunosuppressed host. However, the detection of melanin-containing fungal structures rules out a protozoal etiology. Specific staining demonstrated pheomelanin in the fungi walls (Fontana-Masson) and search for fungi was positive (Gridley).

The presence of pigmented, septate hyphae on direct microscopy, melanin-positive cell walls on special stains, and isolation of Exophiala sp. in culture, fulfilling criteria for this diagnosis, points towards phaeohyphomycosis. Direct mycological examination revealed irregular and septate moniliform hyphae of pigmented fungi.
