Considering the patient's presentation with a widespread rash, fevers, night sweats, headaches, cough, and dyspnea, a systemic fungal infection is a potential consideration. Special stains highlighted numerous ovoid yeast-like organisms consistent with Histoplasma spp. These organisms were more clearly defined using periodic acid-Schiff, Grocott's methenamine silver (Figure 4), and Giemsa stains, supporting a fungal etiology.

However, mycobacterial infection is also a consideration, but it can be excluded because no acid-fast bacilli were detected in any samples. This rules out mycobacterial infection, such as tuberculosis or leprosy.

Viral exanthem is also a consideration, but viral serologies were negative for acute infection. The patient was found to be Cytomegalovirus IgG positive, which indicates past exposure only, further supporting the exclusion of viral exanthem.

Disseminated leishmaniasis is another potential consideration, but the lack of travel after immigration and the absence of Leishmania organisms on tissue stains make this unlikely.

Crusted scabies is also considered, but the skin biopsy revealed fungi rather than Sarcoptes mites, ruling out crusted scabies.

Cutaneous lymphoma is another consideration, but histology showed infective organisms instead of atypical lymphoid infiltrates, ruling out cutaneous lymphoma.

Kaposi’s sarcoma is also considered, but the lesions were not vascular proliferations and the biopsy lacked HHV-8–associated spindle cells, ruling out Kaposi’s sarcoma.

At this point, the differential diagnosis includes systemic fungal infection, and the evidence from the special stains supports a fungal etiology. To further confirm the diagnosis, a second lesional skin biopsy for culture was performed, which grew Histoplasma capsulatum.

Additional pertinent negative laboratory investigations included cerebrospinal fluid cryptococcal antigen, syphilis, Strongyloides, and Schistosoma serologies, which helps to rule out other infections.

Considering all the evidence, the diagnosis of histoplasmosis is confirmed.
