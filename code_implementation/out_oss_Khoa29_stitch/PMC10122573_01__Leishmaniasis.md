The patient's presentation with a circumferential rectal mass, mesorectal extension, and lymphadenopathy on CT scan suggests rectal cancer, with the abdominal CT scan showing a circumferential rectal mass 2.5 cm thick with extension into the mesorectum, thickened mesorectal fascia, and mesorectal lymphadenopathy (stage III); hepatosplenomegaly was also present. The radiology report concluded “rectal cancer plus hepatosplenomegaly.” However, considering the patient's immunosuppression due to a positive HIV test with a CD4 count of 306 cells/µL, HIV-associated opportunistic infections such as lymphoma or fungal disease are considered, but these are not supported by imaging or clinical picture.

Further investigation with an endoscopic biopsy of the rectal lesion and a fine-needle aspiration of the perianal plaque were performed, which showed no feature of malignancy in the biopsy, ruling out carcinoma. The absence of malignant cells on histology is a crucial finding that shifts the focus away from a malignant cause.

The identification of intracellular Leishmania amastigotes in the rectal submucosa and in the perianal lesion confirms leishmaniasis as the cause of the mass-like lesion. Numerous macrophages were stuffed with intracellular Leishmania amastigotes, and FNA from the perianal lesion showed Leishman Donovan bodies (grade+3 amastigotes). This finding is consistent with the patient's immunosuppressed state and the presence of hepatosplenomegaly.

To further confirm the diagnosis, a splenic aspiration was performed, which was negative for Leishmania amastigotes, indicating that the disease is limited to the anorectal region. This finding rules out concurrent visceral leishmaniasis.

Finally, the patient's clinical improvement after treatment with liposomal amphotericin B (and later miltefosine) supports the diagnosis of leishmaniasis. After treatment, rectal bleeding stopped, and his fever recovered, and the perianal mass flattened, with repeated abdominal CT scans showing a reduction in the rectal lesion size.
