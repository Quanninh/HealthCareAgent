Obstructive hydrocephalus from an intraventricular lesion is suspected based on imaging, as the initial head CT shows ventriculomegaly suggestive of obstructive hydrocephalus with obstruction in the posterior third ventricle and an atypical appearance in the third-ventricle region. This suggests that there is a blockage within the ventricles, which is causing the cerebrospinal fluid to accumulate and put pressure on the brain.

Further investigation with MRI after an external ventricular drain is placed reveals migration of a cyst interiorly and superiorly, resulting in restoration of normal cerebrospinal fluid flow. This indicates that the blockage is caused by a cystic lesion, rather than a solid tumor, and that this lesion is removable.

The endoscopic removal of the cyst confirms that the obstructing structure is indeed a cyst, making infectious/parasitic etiologies more likely than neoplastic processes. This is because the cyst can be removed endoscopically, which is not typically possible with solid tumors.

Histopathology of the cyst wall shows portions of a cyst wall with a scolex and hooklets, which is suggestive of neurocysticercosis. This diagnosis is consistent with the presence of a cystic lesion that can be removed endoscopically, and the histopathology findings confirm the presence of a larval parasite.

Therefore, the final diagnosis is neurocysticercosis, which is a parasitic infection caused by the larval stage of the pork tapeworm (Taenia solium). This diagnosis is supported by the presence of a cystic lesion that can be removed endoscopically, and the histopathology findings that show a scolex and hooklets.
