The patient, a 25-year-old male from Paicandu, Paraná, presented with an ulcer on his lower lip that had persisted for 30 days despite treatment for presumed herpes labialis, which had not resulted in healing. The initial consideration was herpes simplex infection due to the location (lower lip) and ulcerative nature, but the lack of response to standard antiviral therapy made this less likely, as the patient was treated as herpes labialis without wound healing or improvement.

However, the lesion's granulomatous, purulent, and crusty contents were atypical for simple bacterial infection, suggesting that a possible bacterial or non-specific inflammatory ulcer was entertained, yet the lesion's characteristics were not consistent with this diagnosis. The patient's frequent fishing in the Ivaí River, an endemic region for Leishmania (Viannia) sp., and several acquaintances acquiring leishmaniasis in the same place, made Leishmaniasis a leading diagnosis due to the patient's exposure to the endemic river and the lesion's morphology consistent with mucosal involvement.

The Montenegro skin test showed a 6 mm induration, supporting delayed-type hypersensitivity to Leishmania antigens, further supporting the diagnosis of Leishmaniasis. Indirect immunofluorescence demonstrated anti-Leishmania IgG antibodies at a titer of 1:80, indicating serologic exposure, which also pointed towards Leishmaniasis.

Direct parasite detection by PCR of lesion scraping amplified a 70-bp kinetoplast DNA fragment, confirming the presence of Leishmania (Viannia) DNA, which is a definitive diagnostic finding for Leishmaniasis. The failure of first-line antimonial therapy (Glucantime) and subsequent response to amphotericin B further corroborated a leishmanial etiology resistant to standard treatment, which is consistent with the diagnosis of Leishmaniasis.

Considering all the evidence, the diagnosis of Leishmaniasis is the most likely explanation for the patient's symptoms and diagnostic findings.
