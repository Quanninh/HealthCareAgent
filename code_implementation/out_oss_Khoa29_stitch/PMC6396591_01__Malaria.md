The presence of thrombocytopenia, elevated inflammatory markers, splenomegaly, and a positive malaria smear indicated acute falciparum malaria. This finding provided a unifying explanation for the patient's symptoms, including the pericarditis.

It was assumed that the pericarditis was the result of involvement of the heart due to severe falciparum malaria. This diagnosis reconciled the initial suspicion of myocardial infarction with the subsequent findings of pericarditis and the presence of malaria. Therefore, the final diagnosis is malaria.
