The patient presents with a 2-year history of blunt ocular trauma, followed by sudden loss of vision in the left eye accompanied by pain and redness, and a gradual loss of vision in the right eye. Visual acuity is hand motion with accurate perception of rays in the left eye and 6/12 in the right eye. Upon examination, circumcorneal congestion and anterior chamber cells graded as 2+ are observed in the left eye, while the right eye demonstrates fibrous proliferation with vascular sheathing suggestive of occlusive vasculitis and vitritis. B-scan ultrasonography of the left eye reveals total retinal detachment with dense vitritis.

Considering the presence of ocular inflammation and vasculitis, we must consider the possibility of an infectious or inflammatory etiology. Laboratory investigations were ordered to identify the cause of these symptoms. Blood investigations revealed positivity for VDRL (1:64) and treponemal pallidum hemagglutinin assay (TPHA), which confirms syphilis as the cause of ocular inflammation and vasculitis.

This diagnosis is supported by the laboratory findings, which indicate a positive treponemal serology. The presence of treponemal antibodies suggests an active syphilis infection, which is consistent with the patient's symptoms of ocular inflammation and vasculitis. Therefore, the final diagnosis is syphilis.
