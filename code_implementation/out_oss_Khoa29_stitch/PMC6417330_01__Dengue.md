In view of the recent epidemic of Dengue in our region, we investigated her for NS1 antigen and Dengue IgM antibodies. However, the absence of positive bacterial cultures and equivocal routine labs made postoperative bacterial sepsis less likely. All routine investigations were done which were equivocal and cultures were negative.

Further investigation revealed a coagulopathy, with an elevated activated partial thromboplastin time (APTT) of 59 seconds and international normalized ratio (INR) of 1.3. She received multiple platelet concentrate transfusions without a notable rise in her platelet counts, suggesting an underlying consumptive process rather than simple postoperative dysregulation. This led us to consider the possibility of a systemic disease causing the coagulopathy.

The presence of positive dengue serologies, including NS1 antigen and IgM, confirmed dengue infection as the cause of the profound thrombocytopenia and bleeding. Both of which turned out to be positive.

The evidence points to dengue infection as the underlying cause of the patient's symptoms, leading to severe thrombocytopenia and bleeding complications.
