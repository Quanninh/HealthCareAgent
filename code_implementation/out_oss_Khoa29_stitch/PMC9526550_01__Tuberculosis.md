The patient presented with a productive cough, fever, and weight loss for four weeks, which started two weeks after discharge from a prior admission for MRSA neck lymphadenitis. He was sent home in good condition, but symptoms persisted with daily high-grade fever and productive cough. This led to the consideration of bacterial pneumonia, particularly MRSA, due to the prior neck infection. However, persistent fever despite vancomycin/clindamycin and later broad-spectrum antibiotics made it unlikely.

The patient's chronic cough and weight loss also raised the possibility of tuberculosis, so empiric therapy was started. However, a direct smear of sputum for acid-fast bacilli and tuberculous mycobacteria PCR was negative, and antituberculosis treatment was later discontinued after getting the negative PCR and AFB smear results.

The demonstration of yeast and the appearance of pseudohyphae and budding yeast cells on the sputum smear, along with cultures growing Candida tropicalis, indicated a fungal infection. This invasive candidiasis was identified, and the patient improved with the addition of Amphotericin.

The invasive Candida infection in a child raised the possibility of underlying primary immunodeficiency. However, immunoglobulins were normal, ruling out humoral deficiencies.

The patient's symptoms and the invasive Candida infection suggested a more severe underlying condition. The oxidative burst measurement with DHR assay showed a markedly reduced result of 0.3% compared to the control range of 98-100%. This significantly reduced oxidative burst distinguished the patient's condition from other immunodeficiencies.
