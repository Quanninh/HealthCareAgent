Initially, a primary benign conjunctival growth, such as a pterygium, was considered due to the fibrovascular appearance and location of the lesion. As the patient presented with a symptomatic lesion on the nasal bulbar conjunctiva within the interpalpebral zone of the right eye, this diagnosis seemed plausible. The lesion was initially diagnosed as a pterygium. However, the recurrence of the lesion after surgical excision prompted reconsideration of a non-degenerative etiology. The history of a prior cutaneous leishmaniasis lesion suggested a possible parasitic cause, as the patient mentioned a small papule of leishmaniasis on her ankle a few months ago, which had been treated with intralesional injections of glucantime. This new information led to a reevaluation of the initial diagnosis, and a parasitic cause became a more likely explanation for the recurrent lesion. Histopathology of the excised tissue showed granulomatous inflammation, which, combined with the presence of intracellular Leishmania parasites, confirmed the parasitic diagnosis and excluded other granulomatous conditions, such as sarcoidosis, and neoplastic processes. The presence of numerous Leishmania parasites within some epithelioid cells further supported the diagnosis of leishmaniasis.
