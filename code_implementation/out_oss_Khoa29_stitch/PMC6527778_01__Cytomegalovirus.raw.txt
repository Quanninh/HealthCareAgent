The patient's presentation with a T-B-NK⁺ SCID phenotype, characterized by profound T and B cell lymphopenia, suggests a classic severe combined immunodeficiency. He was confirmed to have T-B-NK⁺ SCID based on profound T and B cell lymphopenia and a markedly reduced response to phytohemagglutinin (≈2% of normal). 

To determine the underlying cause, we first consider maternal engraftment as a source of lymphocytes. However, there was no maternal engraftment, ruling out this possibility. 

Next, we evaluate the possibility of HIV infection as an acquired cause of lymphopenia. HIV testing was negative, eliminating this option. 

We then consider the possibility of Cartilage-Hair Hypoplasia due to an RMRP variant. A comprehensive SCID genetic panel identified a heterozygous pathogenic RMRP variant, but no second allele and no clinical features of Cartilage-Hair Hypoplasia were present, making this diagnosis unlikely. 

Given the lack of identification of a known RAG1 or RAG2 variant, these common SCID genes are unlikely to be the cause. 

We also consider the possibility of radiosensitive SCID caused by PRKDC, but the identified variant of undetermined significance (VUS) in PRKDC is deemed unlikely to be pathogenic for an autosomal recessive form of T-B-NK⁺ radiosensitive SCID.
