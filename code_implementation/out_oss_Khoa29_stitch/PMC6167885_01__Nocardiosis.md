The patient presents with red erythematous papules surrounding the prior renal transplant scar, with a history of diabetes and hypertension and a recent renal transplantation. Initially, cutaneous tuberculosis was considered, but this diagnosis was made as cutaneous tuberculosis, and the lesions continued to progress with pus discharges, despite two months of anti-tubercular therapy and routine culture revealing no bacterial and fungal growth. This led to the consideration of actinomycosis, deep mycosis, and rifampicin-resistant cutaneous tuberculosis as differential diagnoses. However, actinomycosis is excluded as the organism is not acid-fast and does not produce the characteristic molar-tooth colonies seen on culture. Furthermore, deep mycosis is also excluded because fungal elements were not identified on histopathology or special stains, and aerobic culture yielded bacterial-type colonies rather than fungal growth. Routine culture revealed no bacterial and fungal growth associated with the infection.

Upon further investigation, the presence of weakly acid-fast, right-angled branching filamentous organisms on Ziehl-Neelsen stain, and chalky white colonies with molar-tooth morphology after aerobic incubation, strongly suggests Nocardia infection. Specifically, the phenotypic identification consistent with Nocardia asteroides supports this diagnosis. Right-angled branching, filamentous Acid Fast Bacilli (AFB) was observed on Ziehl-neelson staining, and chalky white adherent colonies were seen on aerobic culture after 72 h of incubation, which turns molar tooth appearance on further incubation.

Based on these findings, the diagnosis of Nocardia infection, also known as nocardiosis, is supported.
