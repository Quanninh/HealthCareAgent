Given the patient's rapid postoperative deterioration, acute bacterial endophthalmitis was initially presumed, and empiric intravitreal injections of 0.01 mg of vancomycin and 0.1 mg of amikacin were administered. However, considering the patient's history of multiple grafts and the observed graft edema and melt, non-infectious graft failure with corneal melting was also considered. The emergence of corneal melting with AC involvement, particularly in the context of multiple ocular surgeries, led to the suspicion of acute surgery-related exogenous endophthalmitis. 

Further investigation revealed that microscopic observation of the corneal specimens with the potassium hydroxide (KOH) preparation revealed mycelial elements, indicating a fungal infection. MALDI-TOF identified *A. oryzae*, confirming an exogenous fungal infection rather than bacterial or sterile causes. This evidence points towards a diagnosis of aspergillosis.
