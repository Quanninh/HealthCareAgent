Considering the patient's presentation of a hyperemic and pruritic lesion on the upper lip that had persisted for six months, Herpes simplex infection was initially considered due to the lesion's characteristics. The patient was treated with acyclovir cream, 5%, five times daily, but unfortunately, there was no response. This lack of improvement suggested alternative etiologies, and a bacterial infection was considered and empirically covered with cefadroxil, given at a dose of 50 mg/kg/day for seven days. However, the lesion worsened, and this failure to resolve the lesion indicated the need for further evaluation.

Given the patient's worsening condition, Cutaneous leishmaniasis became a leading diagnosis, and specific testing was pursued to confirm the suspicion. The Montenegro intradermal test was performed, and the patient was positive for American Tegumentary Leishmaniasis, confirming the diagnosis of leishmaniasis.
