The patient presents with exertional angina, and initial investigations reveal scattered calcifications within the pulmonary parenchyma. Exercise testing and cine-angiography demonstrate ST-segment depression and proximal occlusion of the left diagonal artery, respectively. Transthoracic echocardiography reveals a well-defined, cystic mass measuring 2 × 2 cm on the anterior wall of the left ventricle. The mass is situated between the left main coronary artery and the LAD, appearing calcified with regular borders.

Considering the presence of a cystic cardiac mass, we must consider the possibility of a hydatid cyst. Microbiological and pathological analysis of both the cyst and its ingredients reveals findings consistent with the hydatid cyst. Echinococcal IgG-ELISA test is found to be positive, further supporting this diagnosis.

The definitive diagnosis is established through tissue analysis and serology, which confirm the presence of a hydatid cyst. The patient's history of pulmonary cystectomy and capitonnage for hydatid disease five years earlier also supports this diagnosis. The scattered calcifications within the pulmonary parenchyma and the presence of a cystic mass in the left ventricle are consistent with the spread of the disease.

The presence of the cystic mass in the left ventricle is causing occlusion of the left diagonal artery and the first diagonal branch, leading to exertional angina. The mass is isolated en bloc with the infiltrated coronary segments, and the coronary artery ends left after excision are ligated. Capitonnage is performed after irrigation with hypertonic saline solution, and bypass of the LAD and first diagonal artery is completed.

The final diagnosis is echinococcosis, a parasitic disease caused by the Echinococcus tapeworm. The presence of a hydatid cyst in the left ventricle, along with the patient's history and serological findings, confirms this diagnosis.
