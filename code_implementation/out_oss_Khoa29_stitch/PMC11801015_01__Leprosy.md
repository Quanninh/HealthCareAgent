The patient presents with a 2-week history of diffuse, non-pruritic skin lesions, swelling of the wrist joint, chills, and sensory disturbances (numbness and tingling). He had experienced similar sensory disturbances for the previous year but had not sought medical care. On arrival, he was febrile (38.9°C) and reported severe extremity pain. He denied diarrhea, abdominal pain, cough, dyspnea, chest pain, night sweats, or weight loss. There was no history of allergy, recent sore throat, or similar illness in household contacts.

Vital signs: blood pressure 100/60 mm Hg, pulse 110 beats/min, respiratory rate 22 breaths/min, temperature 38.9°C. Conjunctival pallor was present. Dermatologic examination revealed multiple erythematous nodular lesions with poorly defined borders and infiltrated plaques on the face and both hands, as well as a non-blanching bullous lesion on the right leg (negative Nikolsky sign). A hypopigmented lesion with decreased sensation was noted over the medial aspects of the left leg and thigh. Bilateral ulnar and radial cutaneous nerve enlargement, saddle-nose deformity, madarosis, and olecranon tenderness were observed. The left forearm was swollen, tender, with stretched shiny skin and a central blister.

Considering the classic dermato-neurological manifestations, leprosy is suspected due to the presence of multiple erythematous nodules, hypopigmented anesthetic patches, peripheral nerve thickening, saddle-nose deformity, and madarosis. There were bilateral ulnar and radial cutaneous nerve enlargement, saddle nose, madarosis, and tenderness at the olecranon area, which are all characteristic features of leprosy. The overall description agrees with the WHO definition for leprosy disability grade 1.

However, a bacterial abscess is also considered because of localized swelling, blister, and ultrasound showing a hypoechoic collection suggestive of an abscess. Ultrasound scan of the hand revealed a minimal superficial hypoechoic collection extending from the radial styloid to the metacarpophalangeal joint of the fifth finger, along with increased echogenicity and thickness of the subcutaneous tissue suggestive of an abscess.

Systemic infection, such as cellulitis, is deemed less likely given the absence of systemic signs of infection beyond fever, the chronic sensory disturbances, and the presence of characteristic leprosy skin lesions with loss of sensation. The patient denied any gastrointestinal symptoms, respiratory symptoms, or other systemic signs of infection. There was also a non-blanching bullous lesion, which is a characteristic feature of leprosy.

The skin slit smear was taken for acid-fast bacilli, which turned positive for leprosy, confirming the diagnosis of Mycobacterium leprae infection and establishing lepromatous leprosy.

Based on the clinical presentation and diagnostic findings, the final diagnosis is leprosy.
