The patient's occupational exposure to cattle and unvaccinated sheep in an endemic region, together with systemic symptoms such as intermittent fever, arthralgia, weight loss, hepatosplenomegaly, lymphadenopathy, and multiple pulmonary nodules, all compatible with disseminated brucellosis. Consequently, Brucella infection was suspected.

Further investigation revealed high-titer serum agglutination test (SAT 1:800), positive rose-bengal plate agglutination test (RBPT), and growth of Brucella melitensis in blood culture, demonstrating the etiologic agent. On the fourth day of admission, the serum agglutination test (SAT) result was 1:800, and the rose-bengal plate agglutination test (RBPT) result was positive. Furthermore, the blood culture for Brucella melitensis was positive.

Based on the evidence, the diagnosis of brucellosis is confirmed.
