She was initially suspected to have community-acquired pneumonia treated with antibiotics and systemic steroids with transient improvement; however, her symptoms recurred. This led us to consider secondary hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis (HLH) as a possible diagnosis. She fulfilled 5 out of 8 HLH-2004 diagnostic criteria for HLH with elevated ferritin, low fibrinogen, fever, cytopenia, and elevated sIL-2 receptor. Her physical examination demonstrated chronic limited range of motion of the left wrist and no active synovitis, which made active rheumatoid arthritis as an HLH trigger unlikely. We also considered malignancy as a possible trigger, but no malignancy was identified.

Given the marked hyperferritinemia, recurrent fevers, cytopenias, and lack of response to antibiotics, secondary HLH was a strong consideration. The elevated soluble IL-2 receptor (sIL-2R) supported this suspicion. However, she continued to experience recurrent high-grade fever despite high-dose systemic glucocorticoids, suggesting an underlying infectious trigger. This prompted us to perform a bone marrow biopsy, which revealed multiple fungal elements consistent with histoplasmosis. Subsequent urine and blood cultures confirmed disseminated histoplasmosis, identifying histoplasma as the infectious trigger of HLH.
