The patient presents with a solitary swelling on the right side of his chest wall, which had previously been treated with an incision and drainage of an abscess at the same site. He is not diabetic and is immunocompetent, although he had injected anabolic steroids into the pectoral muscles for bodybuilding in the past. On examination, he has a 5 cm x 5 cm abscess in the right axilla, which is incised and drained under general anaesthesia, and he is discharged with oral amoxicillin-clavulanate 625 mg three times daily for seven days. Routine bacterial culture of the wound shows no significant growth, leading to the consideration of a common bacterial abscess, which is excluded by the repeatedly negative routine bacterial cultures. The patient returns a week later with recurrence of the abscess, and a second incision and drainage is performed, again with no bacterial growth on the swabs.

This pattern of recurrent abscesses in the axillary region suggests hidradenitis suppurativa, which is entertained as a diagnosis. However, histology showing granulomatous inflammation rather than typical follicular occlusion leads to the exclusion of this diagnosis. The histology is reported as "chronic inflammation with multinucleate giant cells along with foci of caseating necrosis surrounded by granulomas", and no acid-fast bacilli are demonstrated on special stains. This pattern of granulomatous inflammation and caseating necrosis is suggestive of tuberculosis, and the diagnosis is considered probable.
