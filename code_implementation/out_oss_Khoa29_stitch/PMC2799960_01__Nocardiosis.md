Considering the patient's presentation with chronic fatigue, dyspnea, hemoptysis, and abdominal pain, along with a history of immune thrombocytopenic purpura (ITP) treated with splenectomy and chronic corticosteroid use, pulmonary malignancy was a consideration because of the large necrotic upper-lobe mass. However, autopsy demonstrated no cancer, excluding this possibility and suggesting that the mass may be caused by an infectious agent.

Further investigation revealed marked leukocytosis, severe thrombocytopenia, rising C-reactive protein, elevated transaminases, lactate dehydrogenase, slight hypocalcemia, ferritin 3817 ng/mL, and laboratory features of hyperthyroidism, all of which are indicative of a disseminated infection. Sputum cultures grew Nocardia sp., confirming the pathogen responsible for both pulmonary and cerebral lesions.

The presence of multiple reticulonodular infiltrates up to 1 cm in diameter bilaterally, a pleural effusion in the horizontal interlobar fissure, and an encapsulated effusion in the right diagonal interlobar fissure on chest radiography, along with an 11 × 7 cm enhancing mass in the right upper lobe with central and peripheral necrosis and multiple irregular hyperdensities throughout both lungs on contrast-enhanced thoracic CT, further supports the diagnosis of a disseminated infection.
