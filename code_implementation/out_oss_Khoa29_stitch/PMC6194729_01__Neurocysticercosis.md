The patient presents with a 4-month history of headache and progressive visual loss, with neurologic examination revealing decreased visual acuity and bilateral papilledema. Brain MRI shows hydrocephalus due to a well-defined cystic lesion located in the fourth ventricle, which is hypointense on T1- and T2-weighted sequences and possesses a peripheral millimetric solid component that is hyperintense on both sequences. The lesion's mobile nature is confirmed by a repeat MRI performed 8 hours later, after a temporary gadolinium injector malfunction, which demonstrates that the lesion had migrated to the opposite side of the fourth ventricle, showing no contrast enhancement. This suggests that the lesion is likely a parasitic cyst rather than a neoplastic lesion, as mobile intraventricular cysts with peripheral solid components and lack of contrast enhancement are characteristic of parasitic cysts.

Further investigation reveals that the intra-operative appearance of the cyst is that of a thin-walled, easily rupturable cyst adhered to ependyma, which is consistent with a vesicular-stage cysticercus. The cyst microdissection was particularly challenging due to its thin wall and partial adherence to the surrounding ependyma, which contributed to an intraventricular cyst rupture.

Definitive histopathology identifies the lesion as a Taenia solium larva in its vesicular stage, confirming neurocysticercosis and excluding other cystic intracranial entities. The pathology report confirmed the cyst to be a T. solium larva in its vesicular stage as had already been suspected.
