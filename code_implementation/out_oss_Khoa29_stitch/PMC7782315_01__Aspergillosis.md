The patient, a 73-year-old woman with a history of acute myeloid leukemia, presented with progressive swelling and ulceration of the nose following a severe cold. The lesions began two months earlier as infiltrated erythema, rapidly enlarged, and ulcerated. Initially, a bacterial skin and subcutaneous infection was considered, but treatment with antibiotics was ineffective. This led to the consideration of a malignant ulcer, which was diagnosed and treated with chemotherapy, but the necrotic ulcer continued to enlarge, arguing against a primary neoplasm. The lesion was then considered a malignant ulcer and chemotherapy was administered, but the necrotic ulcer became progressively enlarged.

Given the clinical appearance and immunosuppression, rhinofacial mucormycosis was suspected. However, histopathology and culture distinguished it from the broad, non-septate hyphae typical of mucormycosis, revealing numerous hyaline and septate hyphae with branches at acute angles were observed. This led to the consideration of invasive aspergillosis, supported by a positive serum galactomannan (1.25 µg/L) and characteristic septate hyphae with acute-angle branching. Furthermore, the biopsied tissue yielded an Aspergillus isolate that was initially identified by morphology as A. flavus.

Other opportunistic fungi, such as Candida and Cryptococcus, were deemed unlikely because serum (1-3)-beta-d-glucan (G) test were all negative, and no yeast forms were seen on histology. This further supported the diagnosis of invasive aspergillosis, which was ultimately confirmed by the positive serum galactomannan and culture growth of Aspergillus flavus.
