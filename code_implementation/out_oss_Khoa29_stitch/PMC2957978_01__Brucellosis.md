The patient's presentation of periorbital pain, headaches, and periodic generalized tonic-clonic seizures, along with a nonfocal neurologic examination, initially raised concerns for a high-grade astrocytoma or primary CNS lymphoma. The computerized tomography scan showed a 5 × 5-cm enhancing mass in the left frontoparietal region associated with midline shift, which mimicked a neoplasm on imaging and frozen section histology suggested these entities. However, the final examination of pathologic specimens showed granulomas with multinucleated giant cells, which led to consideration of tuberculosis despite negative stains. Based on these pathologic findings and concern that the patient may have had tuberculosis, treatment with isoniazid, rifampin, and ethambutol was begun.

Further evaluation of systemic fungal infections, such as histoplasmosis, blastomycosis, coccidioidomycosis, and paracoccidioidomycosis, was warranted because they can produce cerebral granulomas. However, serologic tests for these pathogens were negative, making these unlikely causes of the granulomatous mass. Additionally, a weakly positive IgG titer for Toxoplasma gondii was noted, but the lack of necrotizing lesions and negative stains made it an improbable cause of the granulomatous mass.

On postoperative day 39, fungal tissue cultures became positive for a Brucella spp., preliminarily identified as B. melitensis, and an initial serologic titer for Brucella was positive at 1:160 by tube agglutination assay, correlating with the patient's exposure to unpasteurized cheese. This ultimately confirmed the diagnosis of Brucella infection, also known as neurobrucellosis.
