Considering the patient's recurrent bacterial infections and neutrophilia with left shift and toxic granules, chronic granulomatous disease was considered but excluded because the neutrophil oxidative burst was normal. The neutrophil oxidative index value of DHR (dihydrorhodamine) assay performed for chronic granulomatous disease was within normal range. This rules out chronic granulomatous disease as a potential diagnosis.

Next, we considered general primary immunodeficiency, such as combined T-B-NK deficiency, but deemed it unlikely given normal lymphocyte subsets. Primary immunodeficiency screening workup showed normal T (CD3+), B (CD19+), and NK (CD3-, CD56+) cell frequency, which makes this diagnosis less likely.

Infectious etiologies were also investigated, but were ruled out by exhaustive negative serologies and sterile cultures. Serological tests were all negative. Additionally, the blood and urine culture did not grow any organism. This suggests that the patient's recurrent infections are not due to an infectious cause.

Given the patient's clinical presentation of recurrent bacterial infections and the exclusion of other potential causes, leukocyte adhesion deficiency type I is supported by markedly reduced CD11a/CD18 and CD11b/CD18 expression on granulocytes, consistent with the clinical picture of neutrophilia and recurrent bacterial infections. LAD I analysis showed markedly reduced surface expression of CD11a/CD18 and CD11b/CD18 markers on gated granulocytes, and the frequency of granulocytes having dual expression of these markers was below 1%. This finding is consistent with the diagnosis of leukocyte adhesion deficiency type I.
