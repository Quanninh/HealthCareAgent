Vaccine-induced immune thrombocytopenia (ITP) was initially considered because of recent ChAdOx1 vaccination and isolated thrombocytopenia. The attending physician suspected vaccine-induced ITP and prescribed a course of corticosteroids. However, true thrombocytopenia due to rheumatologic disease was excluded by negative antinuclear factor. There was no evidence of rheumatologic disorder (negative antinuclear factor). Infection-related thrombocytopenia (dengue, syphilis, HIV, hepatitis B/C) was ruled out by negative serologies. Her serologies were negative for dengue (IgM), syphilis, HIV, hepatitis C, and hepatitis B infections. Thrombotic microangiopathy was excluded by normal hemoglobin/hematocrit and Doppler ruling out thrombosis. Arterial and venous doppler of the lower limbs ruled out thrombosis. No evidence of hemoconcentration (hemoglobin 13.9 g/dL, hematocrit 37.4%) was observed. Bleeding or other life-threatening causes were deemed absent, supporting observation rather than urgent intervention. As there was no evidence of bleeding or other life-threatening disorder, she was discharged.

Despite the initial treatment with corticosteroids, the patient persisted with low platelet count (45 × 10^9/L platelets) and therapy was discontinued. This argued against typical ITP, prompting further evaluation. The patient was referred to the Fiocruz institute for further evaluation, where blood count using citrate anticoagulant showed normal platelet count, and peripheral blood smear of the EDTA sample showed platelet aggregates. Pseudothrombocytopenia was diagnosed, indicating in-vitro clumping rather than true thrombocytopenia.
