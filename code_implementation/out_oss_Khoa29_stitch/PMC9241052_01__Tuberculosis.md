Primary bone malignancy with metastasis was initially suspected based on the large destructive pelvic lesion and pulmonary nodules. A heterogeneous enhancing mass in the right upper lobe with scattered bilateral nodules <6 mm, a diffuse permeative appearance of the right iliac bone, and extensive complex septated collections within the right iliopsoas region, deep right gluteal region, and right paraspinal musculature from L4‑S2 were observed on contrast‑enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen, and pelvis. This led to the consideration of a primary bone malignancy with metastasis.

However, Tuberculosis was also considered given the constitutional symptoms and bone involvement. The patient presented with severe low‑back, right hip, and thigh pain, intermittent fever, chills, night sweats, minimally productive cough, exertional dyspnea, fatigue, and a 20‑lb weight loss over one month. The Quantiferon Gold testing, however, was negative, ruling out Tuberculosis.

Typical pyogenic bacterial osteomyelitis was a differential diagnosis because of leukocytosis, elevated inflammatory markers, and abscess formation. Empiric IV ceftriaxone and azithromycin were started for suspected right upper lobe pneumonia, which is a common complication of pyogenic bacterial osteomyelitis.

Endemic fungal infection was also investigated due to pulmonary findings. A BAL fungal workup was performed, but cultures were negative, ruling out endemic fungal infection.

Nocardia infection was not supported; cultures remained negative for mycobacteria and fungi, and there was no branching filamentous organism on pathology. This made Nocardia infection an unlikely diagnosis.

HIV infection was excluded as a predisposing factor by negative serology. This made it less likely that the patient had a opportunistic infection.

Metastatic infection from a distant primary was unlikely after repeated biopsies showed no malignancy. The histopathology of the purulent fluid showed bacterial colonies consistent with Actinomyces and the Splendore‑Hoeppli phenomenon, which is a characteristic feature of Actinomycosis.

In conclusion, the diagnosis of Actinomycosis was made based on the histopathology findings and the exclusion of other potential diagnoses.
