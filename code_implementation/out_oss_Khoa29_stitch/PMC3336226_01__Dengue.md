The patient presented with an acute hemorrhagic stroke, and the first consideration was to rule out an aneurysm as the source of the hemorrhage. CT angiography showed no evidence of an aneurysm, which led to the exclusion of this potential cause. Disseminated intravascular coagulation was also ruled out, as coagulation tests were normal and there was no evidence of disseminated intravascular coagulation.

Further evaluation revealed that there was no evidence of hemolysis or renal impairment, which helped to narrow down the differential diagnosis. Autoimmune vasculitis was also excluded, as tests for systemic lupus erythematosus, antiphospholipid syndrome, and c- and p-ANCA were negative.

The patient's HIV status was also evaluated, and tests for HIV-1, HIV-2, cytomegalovirus, Epstein-Barr virus, herpes simplex virus, and Mycoplasma pneumoniae were all negative on admission and three weeks later. This helped to exclude viral infections as a potential cause of the patient's condition.

Bacterial atypical pneumonia, specifically Mycoplasma pneumoniae, was also excluded, as tests for Mycoplasma pneumoniae infection were all negative. Legionella infection was excluded with a negative urine test for its antigen. Hepatitis viruses were also excluded, as acute hepatitis A, B, and C infections were excluded with repeated serological tests.

Other potential causes, such as dengue and Crimean-Congo hemorrhagic fever, were also ruled out, as the tests for these conditions were negative. Alpha-1-antitrypsin deficiency was also excluded, as serum activity of alpha-1-antitrypsin was normal.

Toxic exposure was also ruled out, as toxic screen was negative. This was further supported by the clinical response to immunoglobulins, G-CSF, steroids, and interleukin-11.
