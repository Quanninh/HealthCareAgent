Considering the patient's presentation with a right leg and low-back pain, and a fixed hard mass in the right lower abdominal quadrant, a hydatid (echinococcal) cyst is considered because the lesion is cystic, multilocular, has a thick calcified wall, and shows fluid-fluid levels on ultrasound, typical of a CE5 hydatid cyst. Based on the US, Doppler US, and pre- and post-contrast abdominal CT findings, a retroperitoneal type CE5 hydatid cyst was diagnosed preoperatively.

However, further investigation is needed to confirm this diagnosis. Upon histological examination, the tumor contained spindle cells and fibroblast-like cells arranged in short fascicles and pleomorphic bizarre histiocyte-like cells, admixed with osteoclast-like multinucleate cells. Immunohistochemically, the tumor cells were positive for CD68 and lysozyme and negative for S-100 and SMA.

These findings suggest that the tumor is not a parasitic cyst, but rather a malignant tumor. The presence of spindle cells, pleomorphic histiocyte-like cells, and osteoclast-like multinucleate cells is characteristic of a malignant fibrous histiocytoma (MFH). The immunohistochemical results further support this diagnosis, as the tumor cells were positive for CD68 and lysozyme, which are markers commonly expressed in MFH.

Therefore, the final diagnosis is a malignant fibrous histiocytoma, which was ultimately identified by histology.
