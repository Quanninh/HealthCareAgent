Considering the patient's presentation with a nine-month history of persistent right hemicraneal headache preceded by photopsia, associated with nausea and photophobia, and her past medical history notable for migraine, a differential diagnosis is formed. The patient's travel history to Morocco and Spain within the previous year, as well as her occupation as a teacher at an orphanage, raises the possibility of exposure to certain infectious agents. A head CT angiography performed seven months before presentation showed a hypodense, right occipital lesion with ill-defined borders and peripheral contrast enhancement, which was later confirmed by brain MRI as a solid cortical formation measuring about 0.7 cm in the right occipital lobe, with an irregular border and surrounding vasogenic edema.

Given the necrotizing granuloma demonstrated by stereotactic biopsy, tuberculosis is considered due to the necrotizing granuloma, the endemic setting, and the initial empiric anti-TB regimen. However, this diagnosis is excluded because Mycobacterium tuberculosis PCR was negative, acid-fast bacilli were not seen, QuantiFERON-TB was negative, and the patient showed no clinical improvement despite appropriate therapy. The negative Mycobacterium tuberculosis tissue polymerase chain reaction (PCR) and the lack of clinical improvement despite empiric treatment for cerebral tuberculosis indicate that this diagnosis is unlikely.

Further workup demonstrated negative results for Histoplasma antibodies, and serum and cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) tests were negative for histoplasmosis, paracoccidioidomycosis, and other fungal infections. Additionally, other bacterial zoonoses, such as Listeria monocytogenes (IgG), Leptospira (IgG-IgM), Hydatidosis (IgG), Borrelia burgdorferi (IgG-IgM), and Bartonella henselae (IgG-IgM), were considered given the patient's travel and occupational exposure, but serologies were uniformly negative. The negative results for these infectious agents make them less likely as the cause of the patient's symptoms.

Autoimmune/inflammatory granulomatous diseases, such as vasculitis and sarcoidosis, were also considered, but were excluded by negative ANCA, ANA, SS-A/SS-B, and the lack of systemic features. The biopsy also showed no evidence of malignancy, making neoplastic processes less likely as the cause of the patient's symptoms.

Despite the extensive workup, the patient's symptoms persisted, and further studies for granulomatosis were conducted. These studies demonstrated a switch in the Brucella panel tests, with positive plate agglutinations, positive Rose Bengal test, and positive (1/100) tube agglutination test, ultimately leading to the diagnosis of brucellosis.
