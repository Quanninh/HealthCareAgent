The patient's presentation is complex, with a history of bilateral Fuchs' endothelial dystrophy and bilateral pseudophakia. Two years previously, he underwent cataract extraction in the right eye complicated by posterior capsule rent and dropped cortical material, resulting in a postoperative best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA) of 6/12. The cornea was hazy with epithelial bedewing, Descemet’s striae, and coin-shaped pigmented keratic precipitates on the endothelium. A right-eye descemet stripping automated endothelial keratoplasty (DSAEK) was performed.

However, the patient subsequently developed steroid-induced glaucoma and underwent right-eye trabeculectomy with mitomycin-C 0.02%. About one week after trabeculectomy, his right-eye visual acuity worsened to 4/60, attributed to early graft rejection. He received intravenous methylprednisolone 500 mg daily for three days, topical dexamethasone hourly, followed by oral prednisolone with a taper. Despite this therapy, his vision further declined to 1/60. A repeat DSAEK was performed for graft failure, and a second course of intravenous methylprednisolone 500 mg daily was administered.

Considering the possibility of early graft rejection causing visual decline, the initial worsening to 4/60 was labeled as “early graft rejection” and treated with high-dose steroids. However, this did not fully explain the rapid decline despite IOP control. He developed steroid-induced glaucoma and underwent right-eye trabeculectomy with mitomycin-C 0.02%. This suggests that the steroid-induced glaucoma may not be the primary cause of the rapid decline.
