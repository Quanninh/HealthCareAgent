Given the patient's exposure to cattle and dogs as well as prolonged ingestion of raw food, the lesion was considered to be most likely hydatid disease of the back muscles. This suspicion was based on the cystic nature of the lesion on CT, which is a characteristic feature of hydatid disease. 

However, histopathologic examination revealed a fibrous cyst wall with macrophage infiltration, lacking the characteristic laminated membranes of hydatid cysts. This finding led to a diagnosis of a pseudocyst rather than echinococcosis. The mass was diagnosed as a pseudocyst formed around liquefied soft tissue, surrounded by a fibrous capsule and histiocytic reaction.
