Considering the patient's presentation of fever and abdominal pain localized to the right upper quadrant, accompanied by a bulging mass, a multiloculated abscess was considered due to the presence of fever, leukocytosis, and a cystic abdominal mass. However, this diagnosis was excluded due to lack of clinical improvement after a full week of appropriate empirical antibiotics, as no significant improvement was observed.

Given the imaging findings of a multiloculated cystic lesion with thick septa, exophytic hydatid cyst (echinococcosis) was considered. However, this diagnosis was excluded because histopathology later demonstrated lymphatic channels rather than parasitic membranes, and all findings were compatible with a diagnosis of CL.

The presence of a multicystic nature of the mass, thin-walled septa, and associated splenic cysts led to the consideration of cystic lymphangioma (CL). Histopathologic study showed large, thin-walled lymphatic channels in loose connective tissue, and all findings were compatible with a diagnosis of CL.
