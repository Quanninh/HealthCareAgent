A diagnosis of ovarian cystic disease was suggested based on radiological findings, as imaging demonstrated a sizable adnexal cystic lesion. However, neoplastic cystic tumor, such as serous or mucinous cystadenoma, was entertained but deemed less likely due to the absence of papillary excrescences and solid components on gross inspection. There were no papillary excrescences.

The Fallopian tube was grossly dilated and filled with similar membranous material, which initially suggested hydrosalpinx or other tubal pathology. Yet, the presence of identical membranous material in both ovary and tube suggested a common process rather than isolated tubal fluid.

Further examination revealed a multiloculated cyst with pearly white membranes, which are characteristic features of a parasitic cyst. This led to the consideration of hydatid cyst (echinococcosis) as the leading diagnosis. Sections show a cyst lined by a lamellar membrane containing protoscolices and hooklets, which is consistent with the diagnosis of a hydatid cyst involving the Ovary and Fallopian tube.

Histopathology confirmed the diagnosis of a hydatid cyst involving the Ovary and Fallopian tube, with microscopic identification of protoscolices and acid-fast hooklets. This confirms the suspicion of echinococcosis, which is a parasitic infection caused by the Echinococcus tapeworm.
