He evolved with recurrent pancytopenia, persistent splenomegaly, and had been hospitalized five times at the IDTNP for kala-azar treatment. Prior therapeutic attempts included pentavalent antimony and liposomal amphotericin B, all without lasting success. This extensive exposure to standard antileishmanial agents without durable remission indicates refractory visceral leishmaniasis. After unsuccessful attempts with all the existing drug treatments against kala-azar, the patient's condition progressed to hypersplenism with clinical and laboratory repercussions, necessitating splenectomy. The splenectomy was performed on August 4, 2015, without intra- or postoperative complications. The resected spleen measured 19.5 × 14.5 × 6 cm and weighed 1,066 g. The histopathological study confirmed the presence of Leishmania parasites within the splenic parenchyma, definitively diagnosing visceral leishmaniasis as the underlying cause of the hematologic abnormalities. Alternative etiologies for pancytopenia, such as hematologic malignancy or other infections, were not supported by the clinical course or investigations, and the resolution of cytopenias after splenectomy further implicates the splenic disease process. He has now been followed for more than four years after surgery, remains asymptomatic, has had no further kala-azar-related hospitalizations or medications, and maintains a normal hematologic profile.
