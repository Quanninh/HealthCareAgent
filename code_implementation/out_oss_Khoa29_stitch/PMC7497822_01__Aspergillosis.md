Invasive fungal sinusitis is considered because of the rapid progressive facial/orbital swelling, vision loss, black crust, and immunocompromised state (diabetes). This is evident from the patient's presentation with a 7-day history of insidious swelling that had gradually progressed, causing nasal blockage, and loss of vision in the left eye for the past 7–10 days. There was no purulent nasal discharge, epistaxis, prior photophobia, diplopia, squint, facial weakness, or facial expression changes. Past medical history was notable for diabetes mellitus (5 years) and hypertension (10 years), with intermittent treatment.

Bacterial sinusitis/orbital cellulitis is deemed less likely due to the absence of purulent discharge, normal leukocyte count, and imaging showing diffuse edema rather than a focal abscess. There was no history of any purulent nasal discharge nor bleeding. General examination was unremarkable except for localized swelling of the left eye and nose; blood pressure was 140/70 mmHg.

A neoplastic process is considered unlikely given the acute onset, lack of a discrete mass on MRI, and presence of necrotic black crust. The imaging description reveals heterogeneously enhanced oedema involving the left periorbital soft tissue, which does not suggest a discrete mass.

Mucormycosis (Rhizopus) is entertained because the KOH mount demonstrated a few broad non-septate hyphae, which are typical for Mucorales. Along with a few broad non-septate hyphae, the KOH mount showed branched septate hyphae of 3-6 microm in diameter with characteristic dichotomous branching.

Aspergillosis is favored as the predominant pathogen due to the presence of branched septate hyphae with dichotomous branching, classic for Aspergillus species. The KOH mount of a nasal specimen showed branched septate hyphae 3–6 µm in diameter with characteristic dichotomous branching, together with a few broad non-septate hyphae.

Based on these findings, a provisional clinical diagnosis of fungal sinusitis was made, and intravenous amphotericin B (1 mg kg⁻¹ day⁻¹) was started. The MRI and contrast-enhanced MRI demonstrated heterogeneously enhancing edema of the left periorbital soft tissues, including the left eyelid, associated with left proptosis and sinusitis.

Therefore, the final diagnosis is aspergillosis.
