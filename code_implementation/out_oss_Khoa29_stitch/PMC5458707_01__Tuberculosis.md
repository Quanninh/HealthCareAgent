We suspected osteochondral injury of the talus and advised magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) and it showed lesions over the tibiotalar joint with joint effusion and cartilage destruction. However, laboratory findings included a white blood cell count with normal values. The erythrocyte sedimentation rate was 30 mm/h and C-reactive protein was slightly elevated, which made acute pyogenic infection less likely. In view of long-standing symptoms and MRI findings, inflammatory/pyogenic arthritis or neoplasm like osteoid osteoma of talus was suspected.

To further investigate, we performed arthroscopic debridement, which provided tissue for definitive testing. The specimen was sent to histopathological analysis and polymerase chain reaction (PCR)-GeneXpert studies. The GeneXpert for specimen showed Mycobacterium TB, without resistance for rifampicin.

This finding confirmed tuberculous arthritis, distinguishing it from the above differentials. The patient's chronic ankle pain with imaging showing cartilage destruction, combined with the MRI findings, had suggested a mechanical lesion, but the laboratory findings and PCR-GeneXpert results ultimately led us to the correct diagnosis.
