The patient presents with multiple painful, erythematous skin lesions over the extremities, face, and abdomen, accompanied by evening-type fever with chills, malaise, body and joint pains, and ankle edema. The patient's history of multiple heterosexual partners and the presence of nasal stuffiness and epistaxis are also noted. On examination, the patient is pale, thin, ill-nourished, and febrile, with diffuse infiltration of the earlobes and multiple erythematous, tender papules and nodules on the face, trunk, and extremities. Sensation to pain and touch is reduced over both feet, and the ulnar, radial cutaneous, lateral popliteal, and posterior tibial nerves on both sides are thickened and tender.

Laboratory studies show microcytic hypochromic anemia, neutrophilic leukocytosis, and an elevated ESR. The VDRL was non-reactive, indicating that syphilis is unlikely. The HBsAg was negative, ruling out hepatitis B infection. The urine analysis was normal, and blood glucose, renal and liver function tests were within normal limits. Chest radiograph and abdominal ultrasound were also normal. The HIV-1 viral load was 1.07 × 10⁵ copies/mL with a CD4 count of 94 cells/µL before ART, but at the time of presentation, the CD4 count had risen to 151 cells/µL.

The patient's symptoms and examination findings are suggestive of leprosy, particularly given the characteristic skin lesions and symmetric peripheral nerve thickening. The slit-skin smear examination for M. leprae revealed a bacteriological index of 4 along with a morphological index of 20%. This supports the suspicion of leprosy. Furthermore, the histopathology of the skin-nodule biopsy demonstrated mild epidermal hyperkeratosis with a grenz zone, papillary dermal edema with macrophage collections, and acute-chronic perivascular inflammatory infiltrate, findings consistent with erythema nodosum leprosum (ENL). This further supports the diagnosis of leprosy.

Tuberculosis is deemed unlikely given the absence of history, normal chest X-ray, and lack of systemic signs. A drug reaction is considered less likely because the lesions coincided with immune reconstitution (rise in CD4) after ART initiation, and specific leprosy findings were present. Therefore, the most likely diagnosis is leprosy.
