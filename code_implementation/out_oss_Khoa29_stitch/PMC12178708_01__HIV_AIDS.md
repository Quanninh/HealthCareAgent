The patient presents with a one-week history of fever and right upper-quadrant abdominal pain, with laboratory studies showing a normal white-cell count with neutrophilia and an elevated C-reactive protein. An enhanced abdominal CT scan demonstrates low-density areas in the right hepatic lobe with peripheral ring enhancement after contrast, suggestive of an abscess. The patient undergoes ultrasound-guided percutaneous drainage, yielding a large volume of pus, and aerobic and anaerobic cultures of the drainage fluid, as well as pre-antibiotic blood cultures, grow Salmonella enterica subsp. enterica serotype Typhi. Susceptibility testing shows the isolate is sensitive to ceftriaxone, and treatment with ceftriaxone 2000 mg daily is started. However, despite ongoing drainage and targeted antimicrobial therapy, the patient remains febrile and begins to develop dyspnea.

Given the persistent fever and new dyspnea despite appropriate anti-Salmonella therapy, an additional or alternative pathogen is suspected. The enlargement of the hepatic lesion and rupture into the pleural space, as revealed by repeat chest and abdominal CT scans on hospital day 14, further supports this suspicion. A thoracentesis is performed, draining 1500 mL of "fish-sauce-like" fluid.

The "fish-sauce-like" pleural fluid and the patient's origin from an amebiasis-endemic area raise suspicion for an amoebic component, prompting further investigation. Molecular testing later confirms Entamoeba histolytica DNA in both pleural and hepatic fluids, establishing concurrent amoebic infection. Microscopic examination of additional drainage fluid reveals trophozoite-like organisms with pseudopods, supporting the amoebic diagnosis.

Considering the presence of both Salmonella Typhi and the amoebic infection, it is clear that the patient has an amoebic liver abscess complicated by Salmonella Typhi and Salmonella Typhimurium bacterial liver abscesses.
