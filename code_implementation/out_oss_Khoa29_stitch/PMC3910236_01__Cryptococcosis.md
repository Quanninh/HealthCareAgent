Considering the patient's presentation with progressive bilateral visual loss and mild confusion, the initial concern was primary CNS vasculitis due to the fundoscopic findings and atypical MRI lesions. However, this was ruled out because neither the conventional angiography nor the biopsy showed definite signs of a vasculitis. This led to consideration of multiple sclerosis relapse, given the patient's known history of relapsing-remitting multiple sclerosis. Yet, the MRI lesions were dot-shaped, diffusion-restricted, and not typical for demyelinating plaques; also, there was a lack of new contrast-enhancing lesions at presentation. This pattern was inconsistent with typical MS relapses.

Further evaluation revealed that the patient's symptoms and MRI findings were also not consistent with progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy (PML) due to natalizumab therapy. The viral PCR results, including John Cunningham virus (JCV) PCR, were negative, and the lesions lacked the typical non-enhancing, confluent white-matter appearance. Herpes simplex virus (HSV) encephalitis was also considered, but the HSV PCR and antibody indices were negative, and the MRI pattern was not consistent with temporal lobe involvement.

Cytomegalovirus (CMV) infection was also considered, but it was excluded by negative CMV PCR and antibody indices. Similarly, Toxoplasma encephalitis was considered in the immunosuppressed patient, but it was excluded by negative toxoplasma antibodies and PCR. Bacterial endocarditis or embolic phenomena were also considered, but transesophageal echocardiography, carotid Doppler, and leg-vein duplex were normal, and cultures were negative.

Autoimmune vasculitis, including ANCA-associated, lupus, and antiphospholipid syndromes, was evaluated, but the autoimmune antibodies and blood sedimentation rate were normal. This led to consideration of a viral etiology, particularly varicella-zoster virus (VZV) infection, given the characteristic necrotizing retinitis and occlusive periarteriitis. This was later supported by PCR positivity for VZV DNA and a marked rise in intrathecal anti-VZV antibody index on follow-up, indicating VZV-associated vasculitis.
