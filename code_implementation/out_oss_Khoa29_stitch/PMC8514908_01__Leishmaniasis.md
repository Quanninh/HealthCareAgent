The morphology of the lesions suggested lymphocutaneous sporotrichosis, skin tuberculosis, or leishmaniasis. To further investigate these possibilities, we examined the skin biopsies for evidence of mycobacterial infection. Fite-Faraco staining did not evidence the presence of alcohol-acid-resistant bacilli, which argues against mycobacterial infection. Additionally, the immunohistochemistry reaction was not positive for BCG, further excluding cutaneous tuberculosis. We also examined the skin biopsies for evidence of fungal infections, but Grocott staining did not evidence fungi, making many fungal infections less likely at this stage. However, we cannot rule out bacterial etiologies, so we performed bacterioscopy, direct fungal examination, and cultures for aerobic and anaerobic bacteria, which were all negative. This rules out bacterial cellulitis or other bacterial etiologies. Despite the negative results, we continued to investigate the possibility of a fungal infection, and skin samples cultured in agar-dextrose Sabouraud medium allowed the growth of a whitish membranous culture, with a blackened peripheral halo, suggesting a fungus belonging to the genus Sporothrix. This identification of Sporothrix on culture confirms lymphocutaneous sporotrichosis as the final diagnosis.
