Considering the patient's presentation with suprapubic discomfort and constipation, the initial suspicion was an ovarian/adnexal cyst, given the ultrasound findings of a cystic mass suggestive of a left adnexal cyst. However, further evaluation with computed tomography revealed a large cystic lesion in the pouch of Douglas with internal enhancing membranes and a "cyst-within-cyst" appearance, which is atypical for solid tumors or inflammatory collections, making a pelvic neoplasm or tubo-ovarian abscess less likely.

The patient's exposure to an endemic region and the characteristic imaging findings, including internal membranes and a "cyst-within-cyst" appearance, raised the suspicion of hydatid disease (echinococcosis). This was further supported by the intra-operative finding of daughter cysts and laminated membranes, which are typical of echinococcosis. Additionally, serology for a parasitic cystic disease endemic to the region was positive, and microscopy showed hooklets, protoscolices, and daughter cysts strongly suggestive of Echinococcus.

The combination of these findings, including the characteristic imaging, intra-operative findings, and serology results, confirms the diagnosis of echinococcosis.
