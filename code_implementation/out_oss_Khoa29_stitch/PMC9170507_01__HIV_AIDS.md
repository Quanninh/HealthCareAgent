Considering the patient's presentation with intermittent epistaxis, nasal obstruction, and weight loss, along with the physical examination findings, a diagnosis of plasmablastic lymphoma is considered based on the morphology of atypical large lymphoid cells with prominent nucleoli and a near-100% Ki-67 index. Histological examination highlighted diffuse infiltration by the atypical medium to large lymphoid cells and exhibited multiple membrane-bound nucleoli, and Ki-67 was almost 100%.

Immunohistochemistry (IHC) showed that the neoplastic cells were diffusely positive for CD79a, MUM-1, and CD56 and focally positive for PAX-5, which is consistent with plasmablastic lymphoma. These cells were negative for CD20, CD30, ALK-1, CD3, CD5, CD10, Bcl-2, Bcl-6, CD138, HMB45, and S-100, further supporting this diagnosis and excluding other B-cell or T-cell lymphomas and melanocytic tumors.

However, the presence of a paravertebral mass raises the possibility of neurogenic tumor involvement, as plasmablastic lymphoma rarely involves that site. CT-guided biopsy of the paravertebral mass was done to rule out possible neurogenic tumor as PBL has rarely been associated with a paravertebral mass.

The results of the paravertebral biopsy were initially inconclusive due to the inadequate sample, but later histopathological examination results revealed ancient schwannoma, thereby excluding lymphoma spread to that region. This finding does not affect the diagnosis of plasmablastic lymphoma in the nasal cavity, which remains supported by the histological and immunohistochemical findings.
