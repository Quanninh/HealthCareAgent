A 63-year-old female farmer with a history of AIDS and discontinued antiretroviral therapy presents with fever, skin ulcers, subcutaneous nodules, and food regurgitation from the nose while eating. The patient's symptoms and immunocompromised status raise concerns for various opportunistic infections. Initially, a bacterial infection is considered due to fever and skin lesions, and empiric cefotaxime/sulbactam is started. However, the patient's blood cultures were reported as negative, and the lesions did not improve, arguing against a bacterial etiology.

Given the negative blood cultures and lack of response to antibiotics, a common fungal infection is considered, and fluconazole is administered. However, serological studies for beta-D-glucan, galactomannan (GM), were negative, and special stains were negative, making fungal infection unlikely.

The patient's subcutaneous nodules also raise concerns for tuberculosis, and a tuberculin skin test is performed. However, interferon-gamma release assay (IGRA) were negative, and acid-fast staining showed no organisms, reducing the likelihood of TB.

Cryptococcosis is also screened due to the patient's immunosuppression, but Cryptococcus neoformans capsule antigen, India ink stain, were all negative in the CSF, excluding cryptococcal disease.

Cytomegalovirus and Epstein-Barr virus infection are assessed, but blood screening for these viruses was also negative, and CSF PCR for CMV and EBV were negative, arguing against these viral infections.

The patient's ulcerative lesions also raise concerns for syphilis, but blood screening for syphilis was also negative, making syphilis improbable.

Nocardiosis is considered as an opportunistic bacterial infection, but tissue periodic acid-Schiff staining, Gram staining, silver staining, and acid-fast staining were all negative, and the lack of response to antibiotics lessened its probability.

Other viral pathogens, including herpes simplex virus, are evaluated, but PCR for herpes simplex virus were all negative in the CSF, ruling out HSV involvement.
