A 61-year-old Vietnamese man with a history of coronary artery disease, peripheral vascular disease, and abdominal aortic aneurysm underwent an axillary-bifemoral bypass and bilateral lower-extremity thromboembolectomy. His pre-operative course was complicated by malaise, productive cough, and a 5-lb weight loss over 5 weeks. He is a smoker, works as an electronics engineer, and reports a cattle field along his route to work. He denies night sweats, tuberculosis exposure, foreign travel, or recent animal contact.

Laboratory studies showed an erythrocyte sedimentation rate >120 mm/hr, white-blood-cell count 18.8 × 10^9/L (82% neutrophils, 12% lymphocytes, 6% monocytes), and hemoglobin 8.5 g/dL. Chest CT revealed bilateral ground-glass opacities, most prominent in the right lower lobe, consistent with pulmonary edema and no mass lesion. Abdominal CT angiography demonstrated a 13.1 × 10 × 5 cm hypodense retroperitoneal mass at L3-L5, extending into both psoas muscles, with adjacent L4 vertebral body bone destruction and sclerosis. MRI confirmed a 13.9 × 5.1 × 10.3 cm lobulated retroperitoneal mass involving the psoas muscles and possible invasion of the infrarenal abdominal aorta and ventral L4 vertebral body, raising concern for an infectious or neoplastic process.

Given the large retroperitoneal mass with bone involvement, malignancy is considered, but two separate core biopsies showed only necrosis without malignant cells. Because there was a high index of suspicion for a malignant process, a second CT-guided core biopsy was performed, which showed no evidence of malignancy.

Tuberculosis is also considered given the vertebral involvement, but it is excluded by negative interferon-γ release assay. QuantiFERON-TB testing was negative, which rules out tuberculosis.

Syphilis (Treponema pallidum) is considered, but it is excluded by negative serology. Serologic testing for Treponema pallidum was negative.

Cryptococcosis and Histoplasmosis are also considered, but they are excluded by negative serology and negative GMS stain. Serologic testing for Cryptococcus and Histoplasma was negative, and Gomori methenamine silver (GMS) stains were negative for fungal organisms.

Coccidioidomycosis is considered, but it is excluded by negative serology. Serologic testing for Coccidioides was negative.

Brucellosis is considered due to cattle exposure, but it is excluded by negative serology. Serologic testing for Brucella was negative.

Despite the exclusion of these infectious etiologies, an infectious etiology is still suspected because of high inflammatory markers, necrotic biopsy, and exposure to livestock. At this point, the overall clinical and histopathological findings raised a high index of suspicion for C. burnetii infection.

Targeted testing for Coxiella burnetii later confirmed infection.
