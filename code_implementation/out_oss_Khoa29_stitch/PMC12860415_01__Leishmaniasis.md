Upon presentation, a 19-year-old melanodermic man from São Tomé and Príncipe, previously healthy, was found to have a progressively enlarging swelling in the left parieto-occipital region over six months after a minor head injury. An initial attempt at drainage at a local hospital was followed by early recurrence, rapid growth, purulent discharge, foul odor, asthenia, anorexia, and weight loss (BMI 17 kg/m²). On admission to a Portuguese hospital, he was cachectic, with left facial edema, ipsilateral neck flexion, and a large ulcerated, painful, bleeding parieto-occipital exophytic mass; neurological examination was normal.

Bacterial abscess was considered due to purulent discharge and foul odor; however, superficial biopsy showed only inflammatory infiltrate and cultures from blood and tissue remained negative, leading to discontinuation of antibiotics. Superficial biopsy of the extracranial mass revealed dense inflammatory infiltrate without neoplastic cells, and bacterial, fungal, and parasitic cultures, as well as PCR for Mycobacterium tuberculosis, nontuberculous mycobacteria, pan-fungal targets, and Leishmania, were all negative.

Fungal infection was entertained because of the ulcerated mass; Grocott stain was negative and fungal cultures did not grow organisms, arguing against this etiology. Immunohistochemistry was negative for fungi (Grocott stain).

Carcinoma (metastatic or primary) was a differential; immunohistochemistry for carcinoma markers (AE1/AE3) was negative, making carcinoma unlikely. Immunohistochemistry was negative for carcinoma (AE1/AE3).

Human herpesvirus-8–associated vascular tumor (Kaposi sarcoma) was considered; HHV-8 staining was negative, excluding it. Immunohistochemistry was negative for HHV-8.

Mycobacterial infection (tuberculosis or nontuberculous) was investigated due to pulmonary nodules and lymphadenopathy; PCR for Mycobacterium tuberculosis and nontuberculous mycobacteria was negative, ruling this out. PCR for Mycobacterium tuberculosis, nontuberculous mycobacteria, pan-fungal targets, and Leishmania, were all negative.

After extensive infectious work-up remained unrevealing, the persistent mass with bone invasion raised suspicion for a primary malignant neoplasm. The patient's symptoms and imaging findings, including a large ulcerated, painful, bleeding parieto-occipital exophytic mass with epidural extension and bone invasion, suggested a malignant process. The mass was later identified as Ewing sarcoma through histopathology and molecular confirmation.
