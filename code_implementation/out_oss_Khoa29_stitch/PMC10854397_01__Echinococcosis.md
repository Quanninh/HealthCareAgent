A 60-year-old female sheep herder presented to the ophthalmology department with a 3-month history of painless protrusion of the left eye and progressive loss of vision in that eye. The patient's visual acuity was 6/18 in the right eye and counting fingers at near distance in the left eye. Exophthalmometry showed a 4-mm discrepancy with a proptosis of 21 mm on the left. Fundus examination revealed a tortuous temporal arcade and grade 4 papilledema in the left eye; the remainder of the exam was unremarkable. Complete blood count was normal. Computed tomography of the brain and orbit demonstrated a well-defined thin-walled, non-enhancing fluid-density cystic mass in the left orbital cavity measuring 2.6 cm × 2.0 cm × 1.9 cm, causing compression and lateral displacement of the globe as well as compression and stretching of the optic nerve. The left medial rectus muscle was not visualized separately from the lesion, and there were no osseous lytic or sclerotic changes of the bony orbit. Abdominal ultrasound and chest X-ray were unremarkable.

The presence of a thin-walled, non-enhancing cystic orbital lesion in a sheep-herding individual raises suspicion for a parasitic cyst such as echinococcosis. A well-defined thin-walled non-enhancing fluid density cystic mass in the left orbital cavity measuring 2.6 cm by 2.0 cm by 1.9 cm, causing compression and lateral displacement of the globe as well as compression and stretching of the optic nerve, is consistent with this suspicion.

However, the absence of bony erosion makes primary bone-destructive tumors or invasive infections less likely. There were no osseous lytic or sclerotic changes of the bony orbit, which suggests that the lesion is not aggressive and is more likely to be a benign cystic process.

Considering these findings, the most likely diagnosis is echinococcosis, a parasitic infection caused by the Echinococcus tapeworm. The patient's occupation as a sheep herder increases her risk of exposure to this parasite, and the imaging findings are consistent with a hydatid cyst, which is a characteristic feature of echinococcosis.

Further investigation, including biopsy, confirmed the diagnosis of echinococcosis. Biopsy result revealed an acellular laminated membrane with germinal epithelium and a daughter cyst with protoscolices, which is consistent with the diagnosis of hydatid cyst.

In conclusion, the patient's presentation and imaging findings, combined with her occupation and the biopsy results, confirm the diagnosis of echinococcosis.
