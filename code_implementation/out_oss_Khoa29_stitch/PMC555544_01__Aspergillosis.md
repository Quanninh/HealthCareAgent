A 5-year-old boy presents with a tender mass in the right upper quadrant, severe dyspnea, a persistent cough, and chest and epigastric pain. Given the fever-like symptoms and imaging findings, a bacterial liver abscess is considered, and empiric cephalexin is initiated. He was placed on antibiotic therapy including cephalexin (100 mg/kg/day). However, further investigation is needed to determine the causative agent.

The microscopic examination of the aspirated material with KOH 10% indicates the presence of branched, septated, and dichotomous mycelia, which is characteristic of Aspergillus. Fungal liver abscess, specifically aspergillosis, is considered as a potential diagnosis.

Upon further examination, the cellophane tape preparations demonstrate septated, branched, and hyaline hyphae with rough-walled conidiophores and radiated conidial heads. Based on these microscopic and macroscopic findings, Aspergillus fumigatus is determined as the causative agent. The rapid growth, bluish-green colonies, rough-walled conidiophores, and radiated conidial heads differentiate it from other molds such as Candida or Zygomycetes. Aspergillus fumigatus is identified by culture morphology, confirming the diagnosis of aspergillosis.
