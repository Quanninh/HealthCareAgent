A 35-year-old Caucasian female presents with ongoing right-iliac pain and intermittent limping of 5 years' duration, without preceding trauma. Given the atypical lytic pelvic lesion in a young adult, a malignant bone tumor is considered. However, the first biopsy was "negative for malignancy yet nondiagnostic," which rules out this possibility.

In addition to the lytic lesion, the patient's symptoms and laboratory studies suggest a possible primary bacterial osteomyelitis. However, the chronic 5-year course without systemic signs and lack of purulence on imaging makes this less likely. Furthermore, the patient's history of a hepatic echinococcal cyst raises the possibility of a parasitic infection, which is a chronic osteolytic process.

Tuberculous osteomyelitis is also considered as a chronic osteolytic process, but it is excluded because cultures were not reported and the patient lacks pulmonary or systemic TB risk factors. Other cystic bone lesions, such as aneurysmal bone cyst, are in the differential but do not explain the progressive osteolysis seen on repeat MRI and lack characteristic imaging features.

Given the patient's history of a hepatic echinococcal cyst, parasitic infection (hydatid disease) remains a plausible explanation for the atypical lytic pelvic lesion. This is further supported by the fact that the second biopsy was "positive for bone CE of the ilium," confirming the diagnosis of echinococcosis.
