The differential diagnoses of thymic cyst, lymphangioma, hydatid cyst and pericardial cyst were considered. Thymic cyst is a possibility because cystic lesions in the anterior mediastinum are common in children; however, the mass extended from the aortic arch to the diaphragm, a location less typical for a thymic cyst. 

Lymphangioma is also a consideration given its cystic nature, but lymphangiomas usually arise in the neck or supraclavicular region and seldom produce a large mediastinal mass compressing the heart and great vessels. 

Hydatid cyst (echinococcosis) is entertained because it can form cystic mediastinal lesions; nevertheless, there was no epidemiologic exposure or serologic evidence reported, and imaging did not show the characteristic daughter cysts or calcifications typical of hydatid disease. 

Pericardial cyst remains plausible as it classically appears as a well-defined, fluid-filled structure adjacent to the pericardium, causing displacement of cardiac chambers without infiltrative features; the CT description of a large, clearly demarcated cystic mass compressing the heart matched this pattern. 

The imaging findings of a large cystic mass in the middle mediastinum extending from just above the aortic arch to the diaphragm, displacing the great vessels, trachea, and heart posteriorly; lung fields were clear and cardiac chambers were of normal size but compressed, further support the consideration of pericardial cyst.
