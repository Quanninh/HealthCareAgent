Considering the patient's presentation of fever, hypotension, and marked asthenia, septic shock was initially entertained due to the presence of fever, hypotension, and leukocytosis. However, blood culture results were available, all negative, making infection unlikely. This led to the consideration of other potential causes of the patient's condition.

Cardiogenic shock due to acute coronary syndrome was entertained because of troponin elevation and low EF. Coronary arteriography, which showed normal coronary circulation, excluded ischemia as a cause. This ruled out cardiogenic shock due to acute coronary syndrome.

The patient's recent SARS-CoV-2 infection and high IgM levels, despite a negative PCR, suggested post-COVID-19 immune-mediated myocarditis. Two weeks prior to admission, a COVID-19 infection, limited to rhinitis and cough, occurred. The COVID-19 IgM antibody test was performed, resulting in high IgM levels. This temporal association with recent infection and the lack of other viral serologies directed focus toward a post-infectious immune mechanism.

Primary viral myocarditis unrelated to COVID-19 was a differential, but the temporal association with recent infection and lack of other viral serologies directed focus toward a post-infectious immune mechanism. The introduction of new cardiotoxic agents prior to presentation was unlikely, making drug-induced myocardial depression less likely.

Considering all the above points, the most likely diagnosis is lymphocytic myocarditis.
