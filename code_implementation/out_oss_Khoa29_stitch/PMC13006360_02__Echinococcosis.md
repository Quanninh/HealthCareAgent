Hepatic hydatid disease (echinococcosis) was a primary consideration due to the large cystic hepatic mass, but it was excluded by negative serology. After liver hydatid disease was ruled out by serological examination, typical hepatic malignancies (e.g., cholangiocarcinoma, hepatocellular carcinoma) were considered because of the massive lesion and elevated CA19-9/CA-125. Yet, they were ruled out by the later immunohistochemical profile that did not match hepatocytic or biliary markers. Immunohistochemistry staining demonstrated that the tumor cells were diffusely positive for HMB45, Melan A, CD68 and CD34 and were negative for desmin. The histological features and the results of IHC were consistent with a diagnosis of hepatic PEComa.

Simple hepatic cyst or hemorrhagic cyst could explain the cystic appearance and dark-red fluid, but the solid component, vascular flow, and markedly elevated tumor markers made a purely benign cyst less likely. Both CA199 and CA125 were elevated in these patients. This led to the consideration of a malignant process, which was further supported by the imaging findings of a large heterogeneous mass with peripheral enhancement on contrast-enhanced ultrasound (CEUS) and contrast-enhanced computed tomography (CECT). The presence of flow signals around the lesion on color Doppler also suggested a vascular component, which is consistent with a diagnosis of hepatic PEComa.
