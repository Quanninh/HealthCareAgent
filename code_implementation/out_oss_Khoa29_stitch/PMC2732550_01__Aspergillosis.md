A 75-year-old Brazilian woman was admitted with progressive memory loss, confusion, and involuntary movements of the right upper arm that had begun after a fall one year earlier. She also had gait disturbance with short steps and frequent loss of balance, leading to a recent diagnosis of Parkinson’s disease that was unresponsive to standard therapy. A computed tomographic (CT) scan of the brain performed on April 30 showed multiple lesions in both cerebral hemispheres, prompting referral.

Given her prior respiratory symptoms and multiple cerebral lesions, a bacterial brain abscess is considered. However, cultures were negative for aerobic and anaerobic bacteria, and the patient was initially treated with 21 days of ceftriaxone, oxacillin, and metronidazole without major improvement. This rules out a bacterial brain abscess as the primary cause.

Another consideration is a mycobacterial (tuberculous) infection, given her night sweats and pulmonary findings. However, cultures were negative for mycobacteria from both the brain and pleural fluids, which excludes this possibility.

A neoplastic process, such as a primary brain tumor or metastasis, is also evaluated due to the multiple lesions. However, laboratory examination did not show neoplastic cells in the surgical specimens, which excludes this possibility.

Other fungal infections, such as Cryptococcus, are considered, but the presence of septate hyphae on microscopy points toward a hyaline mold rather than an encapsulated yeast, supporting Aspergillus-type infection.

Parkinson’s disease, or medication side-effects, could explain the movement disorder but did not account for the cerebral lesions, systemic signs, or imaging findings, thus ruling it out as the primary cause.

Given the presence of septate hyphae on microscopy, the failure of antibacterial therapy, and radiologic improvement with itraconazole, Aspergillus (aspergillosis) is favored. When the fungus was isolated, she was treated with amphotericin B, and MRI showed a decrease in the size of all lesions, which was interpreted as a good response to the itraconazole therapy.
