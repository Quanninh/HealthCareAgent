Acute appendicitis is considered because of localized right iliac fossa pain, guarding, and fever. Examination at that time revealed marked right iliac fossa tenderness in keeping with a diagnosis of acute appendicitis, and a normal appendix was found with multiple enlarged mesenteric lymph nodes. However, the appendix was not visualised on ultrasound, and laboratory studies revealed leukopenia rather than the expected leukocytosis, leading to the exclusion of appendicitis.

Considering the combination of fever, abdominal pain, thrombocytopenia, and leukopenia, dengue fever is suspected. Dengue fever was suspected and was confirmed with a positive IgG and IgM enzyme-linked immunoabsorbent assay.

The laboratory studies showed thrombocytopenia (platelet count 119 × 10^6 mm^-3) and leucopenia (white blood cell count 2.1 × 10^6 mm^-3), which further supports the suspicion of dengue fever. The patient's worsening right iliac fossa pain and persistent leucopenia on the second day also align with the clinical presentation of dengue fever.

The final diagnosis is dengue fever, confirmed by serologic testing with positive IgG and IgM ELISA.
