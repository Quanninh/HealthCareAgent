Considering the symptoms presented by the two male adults, such as fever, myalgia, and shock, viral hemorrhagic fever (e.g., filovirus) is a potential diagnosis. However, filovirus infections were tested for and revealed negative results. This rules out filovirus as a possible cause.

Another consideration is dengue infection, which is characterized by fever, myalgia, thrombocytopenia risk, and hemorrhage. However, dengue serology was negative, which excludes dengue as a possible cause. Similarly, yellow fever, another hemorrhagic virus endemic to the region, was also ruled out by negative laboratory results. Filo-, Yellow Fever and Dengue virus infections were ruled out.

The presence of a vesiculopustular rash in both patients suggests the possibility of small-pox or prior vaccination. However, the patient neither reported nor showed signs of a previous smallpox vaccination. This makes small-pox less likely.

The wound showed minimal signs of local inflammation as the only finding on physical examination, which suggests that the skin lesions may not be caused by a bacterial infection such as staphylococcal. Antibiotics were administered, but the skin findings (centrifugal distribution, mucosal sparing) and progression favored a viral etiology.

Given the symptoms and laboratory results, the most likely diagnosis is a viral infection. The patient's symptoms, including fever, myalgia, and hemorrhagic manifestations, are consistent with a viral hemorrhagic fever. However, the presence of a vesiculopustular rash and the lack of vaccination history point towards a specific viral etiology.

The target diagnosis is monkeypox virus infection, which is confirmed by PCR on vesicular fluid and biopsy sent to the regional reference laboratory.
