He experienced recurrent fevers of 38-39 °C, and daptomycin and Tienam continued to be given to fight infection. This indicates that the current treatment regimen is not effective in resolving the infection, and there may be an underlying cause that is not being addressed.

However, the negative blood cultures make typical bacterial endocarditis unlikely. Blood culture results reported negative on March 2, which suggests that the infection is not caused by a typical bacterial pathogen. This is an important consideration, as it rules out a common cause of endocarditis and suggests that the infection may be caused by a different type of organism.

The detection of Aspergillus DNA by ultra-multiplex PCR points to a fungal etiology. Test results indicated that the patient had endocarditis infected with Aspergillus. This is a significant finding, as it suggests that the infection is caused by a fungal pathogen, rather than a bacterial one.

The pathogen-targeted metagenomic NGS confirmed Aspergillus species in blood. Aspergillus flavus and Aspergillus oryzae were detected by pathogen-targeted metagenomics next-generation sequencing (ptNGS) of the blood. This further supports the finding of Aspergillus infection, and provides more specific information about the type of Aspergillus involved.

The patient presented an aortic root lesion (abscess ulceration with multiple superfluous formations…). This is an important consideration, as it suggests that the Aspergillus infection may have originated from the aortic root lesion.

The patient's persistent fever and multi-organ dysfunction, negative blood cultures, detection of Aspergillus DNA, pathogen-targeted metagenomic NGS results, elevated (1→3)-β-D-glucan test, and imaging findings all support this diagnosis.
