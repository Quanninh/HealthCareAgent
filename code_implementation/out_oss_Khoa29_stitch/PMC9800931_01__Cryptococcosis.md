The patient's presentation with persistent symptoms despite adequate antitubercular therapy raised suspicion for an alternative meningitic process. In August 2021, she was diagnosed with tuberculous meningitis and underwent antituberculosis therapy. However, her symptoms did not disappear. This led to further investigation to determine the underlying cause of her condition.

The cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) profile revealed a protein level of 2,496 mg/L, a glucose level of 1.83 mmol/L, and a white-blood-cell count of 7.4 cells/µL. This profile is typical for fungal meningitis rather than bacterial or viral etiologies, as it shows markedly elevated protein, low glucose, and modest pleocytosis.

The brain magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) showed multiple intracranial lesions with meningeal enhancement, which supports an infectious meningitis, particularly fungal, over neoplastic or vascular causes. This finding is consistent with a fungal infection, which is known to cause meningeal enhancement and multiple lesions.

Further investigation revealed that India ink staining of the CSF revealed Cryptococcus, and CSF culture revealed Cryptococcus neoformans. This definitive identification of Cryptococcus confirmed the fungal etiology of the patient's condition.

The patient's response to antifungal therapy with fluconazole and 5-fluorocytosine was gradual clinical improvement, which further corroborated cryptococcal meningitis. During hospitalization, the consciousness and mental state of the patient gradually improved after antifungal treatment with fluconazole and fluoropyrimidine.

Based on the evidence, the diagnosis of cryptococcosis is confirmed. The patient's persistent symptoms despite antitubercular therapy, the CSF profile, the MRI findings, the definitive identification of Cryptococcus, and the response to antifungal therapy all support this diagnosis.
