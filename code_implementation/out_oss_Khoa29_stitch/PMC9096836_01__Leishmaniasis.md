Considering the patient's severe hyperCKemia and facial swelling, dermatomyositis was initially suspected due to its characteristic presentation. However, this diagnosis was excluded by negative myositis-associated and specific autoantibodies, as well as the lack of inflammatory changes on muscle biopsy. An immunology consult was requested in the suspicion of dermatomyositis, and myositis-associated and specific autoantibodies were negative, the muscle biopsy excluded the inflammatory nature of the muscle disease.

Given the patient's hyperCKemia, a metabolic myopathy, specifically CPT2 deficiency, was investigated. A heterozygous missense variant in the carnitine palmitoyl-transferase II (CPT2) gene was found, which can cause adult-onset stress-induced myopathy. However, the pathogenicity of this variant was uncertain, and it was also present in two of his asymptomatic sisters, suggesting that it may not be the primary cause of the patient's symptoms.

The patient's immunodeficiency was further investigated, and common variable immunodeficiency (CVID) was diagnosed based on hypogammaglobulinemia and lymphocyte abnormalities. This diagnosis explained the patient's susceptibility to infection but did not account for the mucocutaneous lesions themselves. The patient was meeting criteria for CVID, and because of the opportunistic persistent infection, further genetic studies were sought.

The presence of mucocutaneous lesions led to the consideration of a parasitic infection, and histopathology and PCR of laryngeal biopsies demonstrated Leishmania spp. A diagnosis of mucocutaneous leishmaniasis was made, and retrospective PCR of prior eyelid tissue was also positive for Leishmania spp.

However, it is worth noting that an EBV-associated extranodal NK/T-cell lymphoma was later discovered in the soft palate, which occurred after the initial presentation and does not explain the early mucocutaneous findings.

In conclusion, the diagnosis of leishmaniasis is supported by the presence of Leishmania spp. in the laryngeal biopsies and the patient's clinical presentation of mucocutaneous lesions.
