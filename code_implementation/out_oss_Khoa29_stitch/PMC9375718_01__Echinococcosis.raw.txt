Imaging demonstrates a large, multilocular cystic lesion within skeletal muscle, raising suspicion for a parasitic cyst rather than a solid neoplasm. MRI shows a lesion consisting of cystic components within the right vastus lateralis muscle. This presentation is concerning for a parasitic cyst, particularly given the cystic components.

The history of hepatic cystic echinococcosis and prior albendazole therapy increases the pre-test probability of a secondary muscular hydatid cyst. Four months before, the patient was also diagnosed with liver CE, and she had already received albendazole treatment for 3 months preoperatively. This history suggests that the patient may have a systemic infection with Echinococcus, which could explain the presence of a muscular hydatid cyst.

Intra-operative preservation of an intact cyst membrane without spillage suggests a hydatid cyst rather than an abscess or sarcoma, which would usually have friable or solid tissue. Total excision was performed without damaging the cyst membrane. This finding is consistent with a hydatid cyst, which is known to have a delicate membrane that can be easily damaged.

Pathology later confirms daughter cysts and dense hydatid sand, findings characteristic of CE and not compatible with other soft-tissue tumors. The macroscopic examination of the removed cyst shows daughter cysts, and it was found that the hydatid sand structure was denser and more caseified than normal. These findings are highly suggestive of a hydatid cyst caused by Echinococcus, particularly given the patient's history of hepatic cystic echinococcosis.

Based on these findings, the most likely diagnosis is echinococcosis, a parasitic infection caused by the Echinococcus tapeworm. The presence of a muscular hydatid cyst, the preservation of an intact cyst membrane, and the characteristic findings on pathology all support this diagnosis.
