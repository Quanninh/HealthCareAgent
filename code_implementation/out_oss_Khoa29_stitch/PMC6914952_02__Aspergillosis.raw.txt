Malignant sinonasal tumor is considered because the MRI suggested a tumor, but this is excluded by the biopsy showing only inflammatory cell infiltration or partial adhesion of filamentous fungus; we did not confirm evident malignancy. 

Simple bacterial sinusitis or chronic rhinosinusitis with polyps is considered due to sinus opacification, but this is less likely because of marked eosinophilia, elevated nonspecific IgE, and presence of allergic mucin and fungal elements. 

Allergic fungal sinusitis is favored by the combination of extensive sinus opacification, bone thinning, allergic mucin on imaging, eosinophilic inflammation, high IgE, and fungal hyphae on pathology. The CT images showed soft-tissue contrast filled opacification of the left maxillary, frontal, ethmoid, and bilateral sphenoid sinuses with bone thinning of the lamina papyracea and skull base. We examined the resected tissue samples to confirm the presence of inflammatory cell infiltration of eosinophil and neutrophil, Charcot-Leyden crystals, and altered mycelia. 

The hematological findings at the initial diagnosis included 3.5% eosinophil (213.5/µL) and 550 IU/ml nonspecific IgE; specific IgE was positive for fungi (class 2+) and Candida (class 2+), negative for Aspergillus. 

Therefore, the diagnosis of allergic fungal sinusitis is the most likely explanation for the patient's symptoms and findings.
