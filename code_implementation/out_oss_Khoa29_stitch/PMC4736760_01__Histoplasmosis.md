The patient, a 4-year-old boy, presented with a 6-month history of intermittent vague abdominal pain and anorexia. Two weeks before admission, the pain persisted and was accompanied by occasional non-projectile vomiting and nausea, while bowel habits remained normal. One day prior to presentation, he developed projectile bilious vomiting. He was admitted as a case of acute absolute intestinal obstruction, possibly due to intussusception. Initial laboratory studies showed a normal complete blood count, normal liver function tests, and normal renal function tests. Abdominal ultrasound demonstrated reduced bowel peristalsis with no other remarkable findings. A plain abdominal X-ray revealed possible gross dilation of the colon at the hepatic flexure with a few air-fluid levels.

Considering the symptoms of projectile bilious vomiting and imaging suggestive of obstruction, acute intestinal obstruction/intussusception was considered. He was admitted as a patient of acute absolute intestinal obstruction, possibly due to intussusception. However, upon further exploration, a large mesenteric cyst was identified as the anatomic cause of obstruction. On Exploratory Laparotomy, we found a very large (15 cm in diameter) mesenteric cyst within the small-bowel mesentery, located 30 cm proximal to the ileocecal junction and obstructing a 10-cm segment of small bowel proximal to the cyst. The cyst was excised together with resection of the affected bowel and primary anastomosis. Approximately 750 mL of chylous fluid was drained from the cyst.

Histopathological findings (Hematoxylin and Eosin stain, Ziehl-Neelsen stain, and periodic acid-Schiff stain) showed the presence of histoplasmosis spores, thereby excluding bacterial causes such as tuberculosis. This led to the conclusion that the patient's symptoms and findings were due to histoplasmosis, a fungal infection caused by the spores of Histoplasma capsulatum.
