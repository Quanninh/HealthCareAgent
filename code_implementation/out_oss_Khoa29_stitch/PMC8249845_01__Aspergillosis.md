The patient presented with painless gross hematuria persisting for two months, and evaluation revealed a bladder-occupying mass, left hydronephrosis, and left ureteral dilatation. Imaging suggested a bladder mass that could represent various urothelial malignancies or an atypical carcinoma; definitive histology was required to differentiate these possibilities. The presence of a nodular lesion at the vesicoureteric junction measuring approximately 25 mm × 22 mm, with fluid accumulation in the dilated upper ureter and renal pelvis, raised suspicion for an atypical cell carcinoma.

Further evaluation revealed multiple enlarged retroperitoneal lymph nodes and nodes between the liver and stomach, as well as numerous bilateral pulmonary nodules consistent with metastases. The presence of bilateral lung nodules raised the possibility of metastatic disease versus infectious processes; serologic testing excluded common pulmonary infections. Serological examination ruled out tuberculosis or Aspergillus infections.

The patient's condition was complicated by the development of pulmonary infiltrates after immunotherapy, which were considered as either infection or immune-related pneumonitis. However, clinical response to high-dose steroids supported an immunotherapy-related etiology. The patient was suspected to have developed grade 3 immunotherapy-related pneumonia, but after 3 days, the patient's chest tightness symptoms improved, indicating steroid responsiveness.

The imaging studies also demonstrated normal perfusion and glomerular filtration in the right kidney, poor excretion in the right upper urinary tract, and no functional activity in the left kidney. To alleviate left-kidney compression, a percutaneous external renal pelvic drainage tube was placed. Subsequently, cystoscopy was performed and tissue was obtained for histopathologic analysis.

The pathological results of biopsy showed urothelial carcinoma. This diagnosis is consistent with the imaging findings of a bladder mass and the presence of metastatic disease. Therefore, the final diagnosis is bladder urothelial carcinoma.
