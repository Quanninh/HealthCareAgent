CT scans demonstrated a large, heterogeneously enhancing lobulated mass (11.5 × 9.3 cm) with internal necrosis at the pancreaticoduodenal groove, communicating with the duodenal lumen, and a low-attenuation lesion in segment VIII of the liver adjacent to the bile duct and hepatic artery. This dual lesion presentation suggests that we should consider the possibility of a primary gastrointestinal tumor with metastasis versus two synchronous primaries.

Endoscopic appearance of a subepithelial duodenal mass with a fistulous opening suggested a tumor arising from the duodenal wall. An upper gastrointestinal endoscopy revealed a subepithelial mass with a fistulous hole on the second portion of the duodenum. This finding supports the possibility of a primary gastrointestinal tumor, specifically a tumor arising from the duodenal wall.

Duodenal mass biopsy showed spindle cells positive for c-Kit, a hallmark immunophenotype of GIST, supporting the diagnosis of a gastrointestinal stromal tumor. Immunohistochemical staining was positive for c-Kit, but negative for CD34, S-100, and desmin. This finding confirms the presence of a gastrointestinal stromal tumor.

The hepatic lesion was initially presumed to be metastatic GIST based on its co-location and the known high-risk nature of the duodenal tumor. The diagnosis of high-risk GIST with hepatic metastasis was determined. However, this assumption may not be accurate, and further investigation is needed to confirm the nature of the hepatic lesion.

Progression of the hepatic mass despite imatinib, along with a repeat liver biopsy showing adenocarcinoma cells that were c-Kit-negative and CK19-positive, indicated a second primary intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma rather than metastasis. Histopathological examination showed malignant cells with a glandular structure, consistent with adenocarcinoma. Immunohistochemical analysis exhibited c-Kit-negative and cytokeratin 19-positive staining. This finding suggests that the hepatic lesion is not a metastasis from the duodenal tumor, but rather a second primary tumor.

The combination of a GIST and a CK19-positive cholangiocarcinoma established the final diagnosis of synchronous intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma and gastrointestinal stromal tumor. The final diagnosis was synchronous ICC and GIST.
