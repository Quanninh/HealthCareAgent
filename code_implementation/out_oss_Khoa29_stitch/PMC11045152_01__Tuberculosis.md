A 48-year-old woman presented to the surgical outpatient clinic with a painless swelling on the left anterior chest wall below the medial end of the clavicle, persisting for 45 days. She reported an increase in swelling size, pain, and difficulty swallowing over the preceding 15 days. Past medical history was notable for Sjögren syndrome and hypothyroidism. She denied fever, cough, loss of appetite, trauma, and had no significant family history.

Considering the patient's immunocompromised status due to Sjögren syndrome, a cold abscess is considered due to the painless, slowly enlarging subcutaneous mass. Clinical diagnosis of a cold abscess was suspected.

Further investigation with imaging revealed a well-defined heterogeneous hypoechoic lesion measuring 2.7 cm × 2.8 cm in the subcutaneous plane beneath the medial clavicle, with bone destruction of the anterior clavicular cortex and surrounding inflamed, hyperechoic fat. The radiologist suggested an early abscess. A diagnosis of early abscess was suggested.

However, the imaging findings also suggested a pyogenic (bacterial) abscess with osteomyelitis, given the rim-enhancing collection and bone erosion. A diagnosis of abscess with underlying osteomyelitis was suggested.

To further investigate the etiology of the abscess, ultrasound-guided aspiration of the swelling yielded yellow-white material, which was sent for histopathology and microbiology. Histopathology revealed caseous necrosis and acid-fast bacilli, confirming Mycobacterium tuberculosis infection. ZN stain showed many acid-fast bacilli.

The cartridge-based nucleic acid amplification test was also positive for Mycobacterium tuberculosis. These findings strongly suggest a tubercular etiology for the abscess.

Therefore, the diagnosis is tuberculosis.
