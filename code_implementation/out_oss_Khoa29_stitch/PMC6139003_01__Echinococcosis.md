Chronic bacterial osteomyelitis is considered due to the formation of a sinus, purulent discharge, and the isolation of Pseudomonas aeruginosa. The laboratory study isolated Pseudomonas aeruginosa, and appropriate antibiotics were then administered according to the susceptibility test carried out. However, primary bone neoplasm is also entertained because of a well-demarcated cystic bone lesion on X-ray. This possibility is excluded when histology reveals parasitic structures rather than malignant cells. There was no history of previous tuberculosis or bone tumors, which makes tuberculous osteomyelitis less likely. The absence of granulomatous histology further supports this exclusion.

The presence of a well-demarcated cystic bone lesion on X-ray suggests that echinococcosis, or hydatid disease, could be a possibility. Microscopy confirmed the diagnosis and revealed osseous tissue with hyaline and germinative membranes, lymphocytes, and monocytes. This finding is characteristic of echinococcosis, and it is supported by the presence of parasitic structures in the histology. Therefore, the most likely diagnosis is echinococcosis.
