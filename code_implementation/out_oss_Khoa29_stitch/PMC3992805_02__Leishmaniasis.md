The patient presented with a 7-month history of asymptomatic skin lesions that had gradually spread, and physical examination showed multiple bilateral, asymmetric, diffuse papulonodular, non-itchy lesions ranging from 2–25 mm on the face, trunk, and extremities. The lesions began on an arm and later involved the legs, trunk, and face. There were no ulcerations, scarring, lymphadenopathy, or hepatosplenomegaly, and the patient reported no travel to endemic leishmaniasis areas. An initial clinical impression entertained histoplasmosis, and a skin biopsy was obtained to evaluate this possibility. 

Histoplasmosis was considered because disseminated histoplasmosis can cause papulonodular skin lesions in severely immunocompromised patients; however, it was excluded when the skin biopsy did not support this diagnosis. The patient was initially suspected to be suffering from histoplasmosis, but skin biopsy taken from the nodules ruled it out.

Given the patient's known HIV infection and immunocompromised state, other opportunistic infections should be considered. Cutaneous leishmaniasis was a potential consideration, as it can cause papulonodular skin lesions in immunocompromised patients. 

Cutaneous leishmaniasis was confirmed when biopsy demonstrated intracellular Leishmania amastigotes within histiocytic cells, establishing the etiologic agent. Punch biopsy of skin lesions revealed sheets of histiocytic cells in the sub epithelial tissue, stuffed with numerous amastigotes of Leishmania.

Based on the biopsy results, the diagnosis of leishmaniasis is established.
