Considering the patient's presentation with persistent fevers, bilateral flank pain, nausea, and vomiting, along with a history of nonadherence to antiretroviral therapy (ART) and a known HIV infection, broad infectious etiologies are considered. However, cultures remained negative and PCR testing for CMV and parvovirus B19 was negative despite IgG positivity, largely excluding these etiologies. The patient's positive cytomegalovirus (CMV) immunoglobulin G (IgG) and negative CMV and Parvo B19 DNA polymerase chain reaction suggest a past exposure to these viruses, but the absence of current infection makes them less likely.

Further investigation reveals that mycobacterial, fungal, and parasitic infections are also ruled out by the lack of organisms on bone-marrow examination and negative acid-fast/fungal stains. The bone-marrow biopsy and aspirate show no evidence of acid-fast or fungal organisms, supporting this conclusion.

The patient's severe immunosuppression, as indicated by a CD4 count of 27 cells/µL (6%), and lymphadenopathy raise concerns for AIDS-related lymphoma. However, flow cytometry of prior lymph-node biopsies showed no monoclonality in the B cell population, and the current core biopsy was nondiagnostic, making this diagnosis less likely.

The patient's pancytopenia also raises the possibility of a parvovirus-induced aplastic crisis. However, the absence of parvovirus DNA in the marrow and negative PCR argue against this diagnosis, with no evidence of parvovirus infection or malignancy found in the bone-marrow examination.

Given the patient's severe immunosuppression and lymphadenopathy, HHV-8-associated multicentric Castleman disease becomes a leading consideration. Histopathology of the lymph-node biopsy shows interfollicular plasma cells and germinal centers with vascularization and "onion-skin" hyaline vascular appearance, which is characteristic of this condition. HHV-8 immunostaining also shows the presence of HHV-8, further supporting this diagnosis. Additionally, the patient's markedly elevated serum HHV-8 viral load of 2,288,277 copies/mL corroborates this finding, making HHV-8-associated multicentric Castleman disease the most likely diagnosis.
