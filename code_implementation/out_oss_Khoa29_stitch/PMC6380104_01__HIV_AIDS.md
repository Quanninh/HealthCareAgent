The patient presented with a 3-month history of multiple ulcers and a 3-day history of rapidly progressive swelling and loss of vision in the right eye accompanied by fever. Initially, pyomyositis was considered because of multiple expanding ulcers with purulent discharge; however, the lesions' rapid progression to necrotic ulceration and the presence of ocular necrosis were atypical for simple pyomyositis. Before her contact with our team, she had been admitted on account of multiple expanding ulcers with purulent discharge and managed as a case of pyomyositis.

The patient's ocular symptoms, including severe ocular pain, swelling, and necrosis, suggested a presumptive diagnosis of panophthalmitis of the right eye made by the ophthalmologists. However, the concurrent cutaneous necrotic lesions and isolation of a single organism from skin tissue suggested a systemic infectious process rather than a primary intra-ocular infection.

The skin lesions, often beginning as discrete painful papules or pustules, with rapid progression to ulceration, suggested a clinical diagnosis of ecthyma gangrenosum (EG) was entertained by the dermatologists. This pattern is characteristic of EG, and the cultures from the ulcer grew Pseudomonas aeruginosa, the classic pathogen for EG.

The resistance of the isolate to levofloxacin (initial therapy) and susceptibility to cefepime supported a Pseudomonas-driven process requiring targeted antipseudomonal therapy, consistent with EG management. Culture of the specimen yielded growth of Pseudomonas aeruginosa, resistant to levofloxacin, and levofloxacin was discontinued and cefepime prescribed.
