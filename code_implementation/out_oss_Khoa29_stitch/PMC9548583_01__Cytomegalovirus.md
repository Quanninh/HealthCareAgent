Considering the patient's presentation with productive cough, CT infiltrates, and neutrophilic leukocytosis, bacterial pneumonia, such as Pseudomonas, was initially considered. However, this diagnosis was excluded due to the lack of pathogen growth on cultures and failure to improve with broad-spectrum antibiotics, including empirical ceftazidime, imipenem, and intravenous immunoglobulin without good clinical response, and any clinical relevant pathogens were undetected.

Given the high CMV DNA load of 300,000 copies/mL, cytomegalovirus infection was suspected and treated with ganciclovir. However, the patient's fever, respiratory failure, and skin lesions persisted, suggesting that CMV alone did not explain the syndrome. Despite the addition of intravenous vancomycin and ganciclovir for presumed skin and soft tissue infections and cytomegalovirus viremia, respectively, the patient's condition continued to worsen.

Further investigation revealed that the patient's condition kept deteriorating, with fever subsiding soon afterward after alternative therapy, indicating that CMV was not the sole cause of the patient's symptoms. This led to the consideration of other potential pathogens.

Metagenomic sequencing ultimately identified Legionella pneumophila as the causative agent, with high reads in plasma, blood cell layer, and skin. This was confirmed by serology and PCR, which showed positive results for Legionella pneumophila on BALF. The presence of Legionella pneumophila explained the disseminated pneumonia, rhabdomyolysis, and skin involvement.
