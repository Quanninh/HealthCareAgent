Hansen's disease (leprosy) is considered first due to the chronic sensory neuropathy presentation, but it is excluded because there was no cutaneous lesion suggestive of leprosy. Peripheral nerve trunks were not thickened, and the slit skin smear taken from earlobes did not demonstrate any acid-fast bacilli. This rules out Hansen's disease as a potential diagnosis.

Given the neuropathic presentation, diabetic neuropathy is considered, but it is excluded by a normal fasting plasma glucose, which did not reveal any abnormality. This suggests that the patient's symptoms are not related to diabetes.

Metabolic or renal causes of neuropathy, such as uremia or hepatic dysfunction, are also considered, but routine blood count, liver and renal function tests were normal, and did not reveal any abnormality. This further supports the exclusion of metabolic or renal causes.

After consulting a neurologist, HIV-associated distal symmetric polyneuropathy is considered, and the patient is advised to undergo HIV serology. To our surprise, the patient is found to be reactive for HIV-1 with a CD4 count of 160 cells/mm³, together with systemic symptoms such as fever and weight loss, and a high-risk sexual history. This confirms the diagnosis of HIV-associated distal symmetric polyneuropathy.

The patient's presentation of sensory loss and paresthesia over the bilateral lower legs, with diminished ankle jerks and sensory loss to touch, pain, and temperature over the distal parts of the legs and feet bilaterally, is consistent with HIV-associated neuropathy. The absence of other symptoms such as motor weakness, trophic ulcers, or cranial nerve deficits further supports this diagnosis.

Therefore, the final diagnosis is HIV/AIDS.
