Since the patient had visited Delhi during an outbreak of dengue and was found to have a low platelet count, a viral serology test using IgM ELISA for dengue virus was carried out that turned out to be positive. This led to the consideration of dengue infection as a potential cause of the patient's symptoms. However, the patient continued to have fever spikes and chills despite the therapy, which raised suspicion for an additional or alternative etiology.

Given that Delhi is also endemic for malaria, a rapid card test for malaria was also carried out. The Malaria Ag (pLDH/HRP2) card test was positive for both Plasmodium vivax as well as Plasmodium falciparum. This finding suggested that malaria was also a potential cause of the patient's symptoms.

The presence of both dengue serology and malaria microscopy being positive, and the clinical picture (persistent fever, thrombocytopenia) could be explained by co-infection rather than a single pathogen. Blood smears showed ring forms of Plasmodium falciparum, providing definitive evidence of Plasmodium falciparum parasitemia. This suggested that the patient had concurrent dengue fever and malaria.
