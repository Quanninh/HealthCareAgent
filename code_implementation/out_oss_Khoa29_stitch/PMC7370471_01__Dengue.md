The patient presents with persistent isolated thrombocytopenia beyond the typical convalescent phase of dengue, suggesting a secondary process rather than acute dengue-related platelet drop. This is evident from the patient's thrombocytopenia, which ranged from 30 to 45 × 10^3/µL. 

Dengue hemorrhagic fever was considered during the acute phase because of rising hematocrit, pleural effusion, and gall-bladder wall thickening, but bleeding was absent and platelet nadir occurred after the expected leakage phase. Platelet count further dropped to the lowest count of 6 × 10^3/µL on day 7. However, there were no bleeding manifestations during the course of illness.

Drug-induced or vaccine-related thrombocytopenia was excluded by history: no recent MMR vaccination and no platelet-lowering medications before the first low count. There was no recent history of MMR (mumps-measles-rubella) vaccination or use of platelet lowering medications prior to first detection of thrombocytopenia.

Systemic autoimmune thrombocytopenia (e.g., SLE) was ruled out by negative ANA, anti-dsDNA, and antiphospholipid antibodies, and normal ESR. Anti-nuclear antibody, anti-double standard DNA, and anti-phospholipid antibodies were negative.

Infectious secondary causes (Mycoplasma, influenza, CMV, hepatitis B/C, HIV, EBV) were excluded by negative serologies. Mycoplasma, influenza cytomegalovirus, hepatitis B, hepatitis C, human immunodeficiency virus, and Epstein-Barr virus serology were also negative.

Primary bone-marrow failure or infiltrative disease (e.g., leukemia, aplastic anemia) was excluded by a normal marrow aspirate showing intact megakaryocytes and normal trilineage maturation. Bone marrow examination showed megakaryocytes but was otherwise completely normal.

Immunodeficiency was ruled out by normal baseline immunoglobulin and complement levels and lack of historical infections. Baseline immunodeficiency screen (serum immunoglobulins and complement levels) was negative.

The combination of isolated chronic thrombocytopenia, exclusion of secondary causes, and presence of megakaryocytes points toward an immune-mediated peripheral destruction—chronic immune thrombocytopenia (ITP). The clinical evaluation and investigations ruled out differential diagnosis confirming the diagnosis of chronic ITP secondary due to dengue infection.
