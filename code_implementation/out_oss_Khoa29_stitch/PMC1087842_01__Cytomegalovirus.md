This raises the suspicion of CMV infection, which is further supported by the detection of CMV viremia, indicated by the positive CMV culture using MRC5 cells inoculation in the blood but not in the bronchoalveolar lavage. The presence of CMV viremia indicates systemic CMV infection, which is a critical consideration in this immunocompromised patient.

The endoscopic examination showed erythematous gastritis with cardial superficial ulcerations, which raises suspicion for CMV gastritis, especially in an immunocompromised host. This is consistent with the histopathology later confirmed by gastric biopsy, which showed typical intranuclear cytomegalic inclusion bodies, confirming CMV gastric involvement. The diagnosis was first inferred from blood culture and clinical context, highlighting the importance of considering CMV infection in this patient.

To rule out other potential causes of ulcer disease, specific staining and culture were negative for Helicobacter pylori, excluding it as a cause of ulcer disease. Additionally, the diagnosis of Mycobacterium tuberculosis infection was rapidly assessed by an amplification assay and confirmed by BAL cultures, but it did not explain the gastric lesions. The patient also developed hospital-acquired Pseudomonas aeruginosa pneumonia, which contributed to clinical decline but was not linked to the gastric pathology.

Considering the patient's chronic alcoholism, other common ulcer etiologies, such as NSAID use, were also considered but lacked supporting evidence in this case. The combination of HIV-associated immunosuppression, CMV viremia, and the presence of CMV gastritis in the endoscopic examination and histopathology strongly supports the diagnosis of CMV infection as the primary cause of the patient's symptoms.
