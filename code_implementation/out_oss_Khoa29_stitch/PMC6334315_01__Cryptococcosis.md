Considering the patient's presentation with unintentional weight loss and a hard, palpable mass in the left supraclavicular region, lymphoproliferative disease, such as lymphoma, was a primary consideration. This was due to the presence of a supraclavicular lymph-node mass, which is a common site for lymphoma to present. However, histopathology revealed granulomatous inflammation with fungal elements rather than malignant cells, making lymphoma less likely.

Given the granulomatous inflammation and fungal elements, tuberculosis was a primary consideration among granulomatous infections. However, tuberculosis was ruled out by negative BAAR testing, negative Ziehl-Neelsen staining, and the presence of fungal-specific stains. Viral serologies, including HIV, hepatitis, and HTLV, were all negative; acid-alcohol resistant bacillus (BAAR) testing was negative, which further supports the exclusion of tuberculosis.

Other systemic fungal infections, such as histoplasmosis, were also entertained as potential diagnoses. However, mucicarmine positivity, which is characteristic for the polysaccharide capsule of Cryptococcus, made those diagnoses unlikely. Immunohistochemistry was positive for Grocott-methenamine silver nitrate and mucicarmine, which is consistent with the presence of Cryptococcus.

The presence of mucicarmine positivity and the specific fungal stains on tissue led to the consideration of Cryptococcosis as a potential diagnosis. This was further supported by the positive cryptococcal capsular antigen in blood and cerebrospinal fluid, with a titre of 1:32. The final diagnostic was Cryptococcus neoformans var. gattii, confirming the diagnosis of Cryptococcosis.
