A 63-year-old man with a past history of treated hepatitis C presented to the clinic complaining of left testicular swelling of three months' duration. Physical examination revealed a markedly enlarged left testicle; no palpable lymphadenopathy was noted. Scrotal ultrasound demonstrated an enlarged left testicle. Serologic testing was negative for HIV, hepatitis B surface antigen, and hepatitis B core antibody; hepatitis C antibody was positive but hepatitis C PCR was undetectable. Complete blood count, serum lactate dehydrogenase, β-human chorionic gonadotropin, α-fetoprotein, and calcium levels were all within normal limits. He subsequently underwent a radical left orchiectomy; the excised specimen was sent for pathological evaluation.

Considering the patient's presentation of a testicular mass, a germ-cell tumor is a potential consideration, but it is excluded by normal β-hCG and AFP levels, as the remaining laboratory workup including beta-human chorionic gonadotropin, alpha-fetoprotein, were all within normal limits. 

Given the plasmablastic morphology, HIV-associated lymphoma is also considered, but it is excluded by negative HIV serology. 

Furthermore, plasma-cell myeloma/plasmacytoma is considered due to plasmablastic features, but it is excluded by the absence of monoclonal protein on serum-protein electrophoresis, immunofixation, and free light-chain studies, as serum-protein electrophoresis, immunofixation electrophoresis, and free light chain done showed no monoclonal protein.

Metastatic disease from another primary is also considered, but a PET scan showed only mediastinal and hilar lymphadenopathy without distant metastases, and bone marrow biopsy was negative for involvement, indicating that the disease is likely localized.

The immunophenotype of the tumor cells, which were positive for CD38, CD138, MUM1, and negative for CD20, PAX-5, CD3, CD5, CD79a, supports plasmablastic lymphoma over conventional B-cell or T-cell lymphomas.
